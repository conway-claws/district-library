## Woodrow Cummins Elementary

## School Improvement Plan

## 2025-2026

## Areas of Concern-Literacy

Only 58.79% of students met their English Language Arts growth target on the ATLAS assessment. This indicates a need for consistent, systematic instruction aligned with the Science of Reading (SoR) to close foundational skill gaps and increase reading comprehension across grade levels.

## Goal

By May 2026, Woodrow Cummins Elementary will increase the percentage of students meeting their English Language Arts growth target on the ATLAS Assessment from

58.79% to 70%, through the implementation of High-quality instructional materials.
## Strategies

- Use high-quality instructional materials with fidelity daily, such as 95% and Benchmark Reading, as well as Amplify for targeted intervention.
- Teachers and interventionists will participate in district-level training sessions centered on Science of Reading practices and the effective use of aligned instructional programs.
- The instructional coach will engage in district-wide planning efforts and attend professional learning opportunities focused on the Science of Reading and integrating high-quality instructional materials adopted by the district.
- Following the 2025 spring ATLAS assessment, school leaders and the instructional coach will review and interpret the data to identify priority areas for instructional support and enrichment.
- School leaders, instructional coaches, interventionists, and grade-level teams will meet to analyze data from the 2025 ATLAS assessment and identify specific instructional areas for improvement in the upcoming school year.
- The parent facilitator, grade-level representatives, and instructional coach will collaborate to plan a literacy-focused evening event for families, fostering community engagement and celebrating reading.
- Grade-level teams will engage in ongoing collaboration with instructional leaders to design and internalize Science of Reading-aligned lessons using Benchmark Reading and 95% Group resources.
- School Leaders and the instructional coach will conduct walkthroughs to observe and document the fidelity of the literacy practices using High-quality instructional materials.

- Instructional leaders will meet with teachers to reflect on classroom walkthrough observations and use that data to refine instructional practices.
- Educators will track student progress in reading by utilizing formative assessments, progress monitoring using Amplify, and ATLAS interim assessments.
- Educators, alongside administrators and the instructional coach, will examine student data to guide instructional planning and deliver targeted instruction that supports reading development.
- Reading interventionists will participate in Take Flight and Rite Flight professional development.
## Professional Development

- The instructional coach will participate in district-led planning and professional learning opportunities focused on the Science of Reading and the implementation of High-quality instructional materials adopted by the district, including 95%, Benchmark Reading, and Amplify.
- On August 5 and 6, teachers and administrators will engage in district-led professional development centered on curriculum design and instructional strategies. These sessions will emphasize the integration of SoR-based practices and the effective use of district-adopted instructional materials to support student learning.
- Throughout the school year, teachers will collaborate regularly in grade-level teams alongside the instructional coach and school leaders. These meetings will be dedicated to planning and internalizing daily instruction, using Benchmark Reading and 95% group programs as the foundation for literacy instruction.

## Areas of Concern-Behavior

An area of needed improvement is the implementation of a consistent, multi-tiered behavioral support system. Based on observations, staff feedback, and data from office referrals, it is evident that a schoolwide focus on the consistent application of behavior expectations and interventions across all grade levels is essential.

## Goal

By May 2026, staff at Woodrow Cummins Elementary will implement a consistent, multi-tiered behavior support system across all grade levels. This initiative will result in a reduction in student behavior referrals, from 107 referrals as of April 25, 2025, to no more than 100 referrals, as measured by quarterly discipline incident data during the 2025-2026 school year.

### Strategies

- Administrators and the school counselor will attend the Arkansas School Behavior Conference to gather strategies, tools, and insights for strengthening schoolwide behavior initiatives.
- Administrators will establish a Behavioral Leadership Team responsible for guiding the implementation and continuous improvement of the multi-tiered system of behavioral supports.
- All staff will participate in professional development sessions focused on implementing consistent behavior practices across all settings. These trainings will include a reevaluation of the campus-wide behavior matrix and aligned expectations.
- Staff will proactively teach, model, and reinforce the shared behavior expectations outlined in the school’s behavior matrix to ensure student understanding at both the classroom and schoolwide levels.
- The school counselor will support teachers in integrating social-emotional learning (SEL) instruction into their classrooms.
- Staff will implement classroom-level interventions. If a student continues to struggle, a referral will be made to the Behavior RTI Committee to identify additional supports and interventions.
- Staff will implement Tier II supports recommended by the Behavior RTI Committee and track student progress to determine the effectiveness of the interventions.

- Administrators and the school counselor will collaborate to analyze data from Tier II interventions, making adjustments as necessary based on student response.
- When a student requires more intensive behavioral support, the school counselor will conduct a Functional Behavior Assessment (FBA) and develop a Behavior Intervention Plan (BIP) as needed.
- Administrators will collaborate with the Behavioral Leadership Team to plan behavior incentive events that recognize and reward students who consistently demonstrate positive behaviors.
### Professional Development

- In June, the administrative team and school counselor will participate in district-sponsored professional learning focused on the design and consistent implementation of a multi-tiered system of behavioral supports.
- In July, administrators and the school counselor will attend the Arkansas School Behavior Conference to gain new tools and strategies for leading school behavior teams and enhancing student support systems.
- During Back-to-School Professional Development in August, all faculty and staff will engage in training to deepen their understanding of the multi-tiered behavioral framework. This session will include a refresher on the school’s behavior matrix and reinforce consistent expectations for student behavior across all environments.
