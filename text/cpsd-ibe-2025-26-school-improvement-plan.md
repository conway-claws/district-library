## 2025-2026 School Improvement Plan Ida Burns Elementary School

## <u>Area of Concern</u> (#1)

A significant area of concern at Ida Burns Elementary is our current literacy proficiency levels, particularly in grades three and four. Based on our most recent assessment data, only 46% of our third-grade students and 45% of our fourth-grade students are performing at or above grade-level expectations in literacy. These percentages reflect a troubling trend, especially given the foundational importance of reading proficiency by the end of third grade—a milestone often associated with future academic success.

## <u>Goal</u> (#1)

By the end of the 2025-2026 school year, Ida Burns Elementary will increase the percentage of third and fourth grade students scoring Level 3 and Level 4 on the ATLAS summative literacy assessment by 10 percentage points, raising third grade proficiency from 46% to 56% and fourth grade proficiency from 45% to 55%.

## <u>Strategies/Action Steps</u>

Teachers will use screeners, as well as, informal assessments to target their instruction. They will be able to know exactly what gaps in phonological awareness and phonics students have in order to provide the best explicit individualized instruction. These assessments will be analyzed on a regular basis and adjustments in instruction will be made according to what the data shows. Teachers will keep Data notebooks with student anecdotal notes, checklists, accuracy checks, and other informal assessments along with screeners and formal assessment measures. Teachers in all grade levels will provide Tier II intervention for Literacy during RTI time daily, as well as pull out intervention available with school Interventionists. Teachers will teach the essential lessons from Benchmark Reading and 95% Phonics as outlined in unit plans. Teachers will communicate with families to support reading instruction at home. RISE-Grades K-4 will fully implement and follow RISE, including the use of decodable texts to target specific reading levels. Teachers will meet weekly during PLC to collaborate within grade level to implement strong Tier I instruction, provide common formative assessments, reflect on data, lesson internalize, and pre-plan for instruction. The instructional coach will participate in district-level planning and professional development sessions aligned to the SoR and the implementation of

district-adopted HQIMs. Teachers will attend professional development offered by the district on curriculum and instruction aligned to the SoR and implementation of HQIM aligned to SoR. The administrators and instructional coach will conduct classroom walkthroughs using DESE’s *key levers for instructional leaders* to gather evidence of implementation as it relates to the five impact areas in literacy. <u>Professional Development</u> Our teachers will attend Curriculum Planning days facilitated by our District instructional Facilitators. During this planning time they will be gaining knowledge about our district Tier I curriculum for Literacy (Benchmark Reading, 95% Phonics, and Writing). This allows for our teachers to understand the pacing of our curriculum and to understand how to teach it with fidelity. It also allows for planning among our district grade level teams.

Our RTI Team analyzes data on struggling students every 4-6 weeks in order to make sure that we are serving all students to the best of our ability. This allows us to see if a student is not making progress, or if another intervention is needed in order to help them move to the next part of their learning.

Based on the needs of students, Admin and the Instructional Facilitator will determine if additional learning is needed for the teacher, in order to best meet the student needs.

- Progress Monitoring through Acadience

## <u>Area of Concern</u> (#2)

A growing area of concern at Ida Burns Elementary is the inconsistent implementation and monitoring of our Tier I, Tier II, and Tier III behavior systems across the building. While foundational expectations have been established school-wide, there is a noticeable gap in consistency, follow-through, and fidelity in how behavioral supports are delivered and documented across classrooms and grade levels.

## <u>Goal</u> (#2)

By the end of the 2024–2025 school year, Ida Burns Elementary will implement Tier I, Tier II, and Tier III behavioral supports with fidelity, as measured by:

- A 20% reduction in office discipline referrals (ODRs) from the previous school year,
- 100% of classrooms implementing Tier I expectations with consistency, as verified by monthly walkthrough checklists,
- And all Tier II and Tier III student support plans are tracked with weekly progress monitoring and reviewed at least monthly by the PBIS/behavioral support team.
## <u>Strategies/Action Steps</u>

Teachers will implement a school wide behavior plan. Teachers will make positive phone calls home quarterly. Teachers will participate in professional development focused on trauma. Teachers will implement a “Calm Down corner.” A discipline committee will meet quarterly to discuss school wide behavior. A positive behavior reward system will be implemented. Classes receive counseling lessons monthly. Ida Burns Staff will plan and conduct two parents nights to include our families in a social environment. Monthly awards ceremonies. Behavior flowchart.
