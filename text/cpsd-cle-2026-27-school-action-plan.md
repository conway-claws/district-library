## SCHOOL ACTION PLAN

#### 2026–2027

*Aligned to the Conway Public Schools District Strategic Reading Plan*

*and the Conway Public School District Math Plan*

### AREA OF CONCERN / NEED #1: LITERACY

##### Background and Data Context

In 2024–2025, Carolyn Lewis Elementary (CLE) students demonstrated reading proficiency that exceeded district and state benchmarks. On the Spring 2025 ATLAS Summative Assessment, 49% of CLE third-grade students achieved proficiency or advanced status in literacy, compared to 27% for the Conway district and 36% for the state. At fourth grade, 54% of CLE students achieved proficiency or advanced status, compared to 46% for the district and 39% for the state. While these results reflect meaningful progress and positional strength, roughly half of all students in each grade remain below proficiency, and the gap between third and fourth grade indicates a continuing need for targeted early-grade intervention and instructional consistency.

The Conway Public Schools District Strategic Reading Plan establishes an ambitious district-wide goal of raising reading proficiency from 41% (2024–2025) to 80% by the 2029–2030 school year, requiring an average increase of approximately 8 percentage points per year across the district. CLE’s 2026–2027 action plan aligns with the district plan’s Year 2 priorities while building upon the foundational implementation work completed during 2025–2026.

#### GOAL #1

By the end of the 2026–2027 school year, the percentage of CLE students achieving proficiency or advanced status in literacy, as measured by the ATLAS Summative Assessment, will increase by at least 8 percentage points in each grade level, consistent with the Conway Public Schools District Strategic Reading Plan’s annual growth target. Specifically, CLE will target a minimum of 57% proficiency in 3rd grade and 62% proficiency in 4th grade. This goal is supported by the full and faithful implementation of the newly adopted HQIM Tier 1 reading program, continued Tier 2 and Tier 3 Scottish Rite interventions, data-driven instruction, and ongoing progress monitoring.

##### <u>Alignment to District Strategic Reading Plan — Year 2 Priorities</u>

The following strategies directly correspond to the District Strategic Reading Plan’s Year 2 (2026–2027) action items across the 10 Pillars for Reading Excellence:

#### STRATEGIES

##### <u>Pillar I: High-Quality Curriculum and Instruction (HQIM)</u>

Fully implement the new district-adopted HQIM Tier 1 reading program with fidelity, following the completion of the HQIM pilot and selection process in 2025–2026. All K–4 teachers will receive professional development and instructional coaching in the new program prior to and during implementation. Implementation of Arts and Letters for comprehension and fluency components as applicable and aligned to the new HQIM framework. Continue full implementation of 95% Group Phonics instruction in grades K–4 with fidelity as paced by the curriculum. Protect and maintain dedicated instructional time for reading. Building schedules and intervention block schedules will be reviewed for efficiency. Administrators and the Instructional Coach will support teachers through Lesson Internalization practices to ensure intentional and effective delivery of literacy instruction aligned to student needs.

##### <u>Pillar II: Curriculum Coherence and Vertical Alignment</u>

Embed literacy across content areas including science and social studies, in alignment with the district’s cross-curricular literacy approach. Teachers will use the district-provided content area literacy resources. Participate in vertical alignment collaborative planning to ensure continuity in reading instruction from kindergarten through fourth grade and into fifth grade.

##### <u>Pillar III: Early Literacy Intervention</u>

Utilize DIBELS assessment data to identify students at risk and place them in appropriate WIN (What I Need) intervention groups in grades K–4. Implement the WIN block daily, ensuring targeted, differentiated interventions at all levels—below, at, and above benchmark—in the classroom and through pull-out support as needed. Implement Amplify Tier 2 Intervention Curriculum with fidelity in grades K–4 and with Activity Teachers. Continue graduated implementation of Scottish Rite intervention programs: Build (K–2 Tier 2), Rite Flight (Grade 3+ Tier 2), and Take Flight (Grade 2+ Tier 3) as trained and as appropriate to individual student need. Sonday will continue to be utilized based on student need and teacher training status.

##### <u>Pillar IV: Professional Development and Instructional Support</u>

Provide comprehensive professional development in the new HQIM Tier 1 program for all teachers not previously trained, ensuring full readiness for implementation. Continue Science of Reading professional development, with ongoing support from Administrators and Instructional Facilitators. Provide continuous coaching and professional development in Amplify Tier 2 Interventions. Support interventionists and teachers with ongoing professional development in Scottish Rite programs (Build, Rite Flight, Take Flight). Teachers will participate in weekly PLC and collaboration meetings to plan, internalize lessons, and review data with the Instructional Coach and Administrators.

##### <u>Pillar VII: Family and Community Engagement</u>

Provide Individualized Reading Progress (IRP) reports for Tier 3 students to parents each nine weeks, in parent-friendly language. Embed literacy resources and information in community events and on the school website, aligned to district literacy resources for families. Communicate student reading progress clearly with families, using parent-friendly interpretations of DIBELS and ATLAS data.

##### <u>Pillar VIII: Assessment and Progress Monitoring</u>

Administer DIBELS screener consistently three times per year (BOY, MOY, EOY) to identify at-risk students early. Monitor all students through the data wall with monthly progress monitoring updates. Adjustments to intervention groups will be made on an as-needed, individual basis. Analyze interim ATLAS assessment data each nine weeks and adjust instruction accordingly.

##### <u>Pillar IX: Data-Driven Decision Making</u>

Utilize the RTI process to ensure students are making progress from their at-level interventions. If a lack of progress is observed, adjustments will be made to the interventions provided. The data team will monitor all student progress and determine who is brought to the RTI committee for additional support. Teachers will review data and plan appropriately during weekly collaboration meetings and scheduled RTI meetings. Data analysis PLCs will occur in which teachers collaboratively review student data and co-plan targeted interventions.

##### <u>Pillar X: Sustainability and Continuous Improvement</u>

Monitor compliance with state dyslexia laws and Child Find mandates in coordination with district special education leadership. Participate in the structured district Instructional Leadership Team (ILT) review process to support ongoing plan updates and accountability.

#### PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT

New HQIM Tier 1 reading program training (summer 2026 and ongoing throughout the school year) for all K–4 teachers. Science of Reading: continuous support from Administrators and Instructional Facilitators. UFLI, Amplify, BUILD, and Rite Flight Tier 2 Intervention: ongoing professional development and coaching. Scottish Rite programs (Build, Rite Flight, Take Flight): ongoing training and implementation support. RTI process and data analysis: training integrated into weekly collaboration and PLC structures. Lesson internalization and instructional delivery: ongoing coaching cycles with Instructional Coach and Administrators.

#### IMPLEMENTATION PLAN

Summer 2026: HQIM Tier 1 professional development for all staff. Schedules reviewed for intervention block efficiency. WIN groups planned based on BOY DIBELS data. August 2026: Administer BOY DIBELS screener. Form WIN groups. Launch Amplify Tier 2 groups. Begin HQIM Tier 1 implementation. September–October 2026: Scottish Rite intervention groups launched based on trained staff and screener data. RTI committee convenes. Data wall updated. Ongoing monthly: Data wall updated with DIBELS and progress monitoring. RTI meetings review student progress. Adjustments to intervention groups made as needed. Each nine weeks: Interim ATLAS data analyzed. IRP reports distributed to families of Tier 3 students. ILT review process meetings attended. Spring 2027: Administer EOY DIBELS and ATLAS Summative. Data analyzed to determine goal attainment.

#### EVALUATION

Administrators and Instructional Facilitators will analyze classroom progress monitoring results and adjust instruction accordingly. Interim ATLAS assessment data will be analyzed each nine weeks and instruction adjusted accordingly. Spring 2027 ATLAS Summative data will be analyzed to determine whether proficiency targets (57% in 3rd grade; 62% in 4th grade) were met. Data will be shared with the district C&I committee as part of the structured plan review process.

### AREA OF CONCERN / NEED #2: MATHEMATICS

##### Background and Data Context

On the Spring 2025 ATLAS Summative Assessment, CLE third-grade students achieved 44% proficiency or advanced status in mathematics, compared to 37% for the Conway district and 39% for the state. Fourth-grade CLE students achieved 55% proficiency or advanced status, compared to 43% for both the district and state. While CLE outperforms the district and state averages at both grade levels, proficiency gaps remain significant, particularly in third grade, and align with district-wide concerns in foundational math skill development.

The Conway Public School District Math Plan establishes a goal of raising district-wide math proficiency from 38% (2025 baseline) to 43% by 2026 and 60% or higher by 2030. The Plan’s Year 2 (2026–2027) focus is on Acceleration Pathways and HQIM Review, emphasizing vertical alignment, targeted intervention, honors pathway preparation, and deepened instructional practice. CLE’s mathematics action plan aligns directly with these district priorities.

District-identified skill deficits from the 2025 ATLAS applicable to CLE’s elementary grades include: Fractions → Ratios → Proportional Reasoning → Slope (the #1 cross-grade challenge, Grades 3–Algebra I) Data Literacy Across Grades (3rd-grade graph scaling, picture graphs, line plots) Geometry Vocabulary and Spatial Reasoning (Grades 3–5) Translating Real-World Problems to Mathematical Models (affecting multi-step problem solving and early operations) Equation Solving Fluency (foundational gaps beginning in Grades 3–5)

#### GOAL #2

By the end of the 2026–2027 school year, the percentage of CLE students achieving proficiency or advanced status in mathematics, as measured by the ATLAS Summative Assessment, will increase by at least 5 percentage points in each grade level, targeting a minimum of 49% proficiency in 3rd grade and 60% proficiency in 4th grade. This goal will be supported through fidelity to Eureka Math², targeted Tier 2 interventions, data-driven instruction, and alignment to the five district-identified skill deficit strands.

##### <u>Alignment to District Math Plan — Year 2 (2026–2027) Priorities</u>

The District Math Plan’s Year 2 focus is Acceleration Pathways and HQIM Review. For CLE as an elementary school, this translates to: Strengthening foundational skill development in Grades 3–4 to prepare students for the honors and acceleration pathways beginning in Grade 5. Refining Tier 2 intervention practices using common formative assessments aligned to district math skill deficit strands. Building teacher capacity through ongoing professional development and coaching.

#### STRATEGIES

##### <u>High-Quality Instructional Materials (HQIM) — Eureka Math²</u>

Continue full and faithful implementation of Eureka Math² in grades K–4, consistent with the district’s HQIM framework. Administrators and instructional coaches will conduct focused math look-fors to support instructional fidelity and identify areas for growth. Participate in the district’s HQIM review process with the math specialist and instructional coaches to evaluate and adjust materials as necessary. Utilize Eureka Math Equip diagnostic assessments (K–7) to identify individual student learning gaps and tailor instruction accordingly, with a focus on the five district-identified skill deficit strands.

##### <u>Intervention and Data-Driven Instruction</u>

Identify students performing at the lowest quartile on ATLAS and interim assessments. Provide these students with appropriate Tier 2 small-group instruction during WIN time and in-class intervention blocks. Develop and maintain Math Intervention Plans (MIPs) for all students who scored Level 1 on the 2025 ATLAS, in compliance with Arkansas Code Annotated § 6-17-431. MIPs will be reviewed and updated based on interim assessment data. Use Eureka Math Equip formative data to identify skill deficits, monitor progress, and adjust instruction in real time. Implement the Modern Classroom Project framework (layered with Eureka Math²) in participating classrooms to support small group instruction, student goal setting, and mastery-based learning. Utilize the RTI process for students not making expected progress in math. Adjustments to intervention groups and strategies will be made on an as-needed, individual basis.

##### <u>Vertical Alignment and Instructional Practice</u>

Participate in district vertical alignment meetings to standardize math vocabulary and instructional practices across grade levels, addressing identified barriers and supporting students’ transition toward the honors pathway beginning in Grade 5. Incorporate math discourse routines including number talks, turn-and-talks, and error analysis conversations aligned with Building Thinking Classrooms practices. Ensure instructional time is protected for math, and that WIN blocks include structured, standards-aligned math intervention alongside literacy intervention.

##### <u>Focus on District-Identified Skill Deficit Strands</u>

Instruction and intervention will explicitly address the five district skill deficit strands at the elementary level: Fractions: foundational fraction concepts (identifying, comparing, and placing fractions on a number line) in grades 3–4. Data Literacy: creating and interpreting scaled bar graphs, picture graphs, and line plots in grade 3; data analysis in grade 4. Geometry Vocabulary and Spatial Reasoning: quadrilateral identification, geometric measurement, and spatial reasoning in grades 3–4. Real-World Problem Solving: multi-step word problems using the four operations, with attention to equation writing and unknown quantities. Equation Fluency: building fluency with addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division as foundational preparation for algebraic thinking.

##### <u>Family and Community Engagement</u>

Distribute parent-friendly fluency guides (K–2 and 3–5 Math Fluency documents) via the school website and family communications, consistent with the district math plan’s family engagement resources. Communicate student math progress clearly with families, using parent-friendly interpretations of Eureka Math Equip and ATLAS data. Incorporate math awareness into family events and school communications.

#### PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT

Ongoing professional development in Eureka Math² instructional strategies, with a focus on the five district skill deficit strands and effective Tier 1 instructional practices. Professional Development opportunities with the district Math Academy. Training in common formative assessment development and data analysis as part of the district’s Tier 2 refinement process. Modern Classroom Project professional development for participating teachers, with ongoing coaching support from the secondary curriculum coordinator. Differentiated instruction for students with IEPs and English learners, aligned to district-provided professional learning opportunities. Math walk-throughs and instructional coaching cycles with Administrators and district Instructional Coaches throughout the year.

#### IMPLEMENTATION PLAN

Summer 2026: Review ATLAS Summative data by student and standard. Update Math Intervention Plans. Prepare WIN block schedules to include math intervention. Attend district-provided professional learning. August 2026: Launch Eureka Math² with full fidelity. Administer Eureka Math Equip diagnostics. Identify lowest-quartile students for targeted support. Establish WIN groups for math. September–October 2026: RTI committee convenes for math. MIPs updated and implemented. Math look-fors conducted by Administrators and Instructional Coaches. Pear Assessment used for formative monitoring.

Ongoing monthly: Data reviewed at collaboration and RTI meetings. WIN groups adjusted based on progress monitoring. Learning ladders shared with students and families. Each nine weeks: Interim ATLAS data analyzed. MIPs reviewed and updated. Data shared with the district C&I committee and math coalition. Spring 2027: ATLAS Summative administered. Data analyzed to determine goal attainment and inform 2027–2028 planning.

#### EVALUATION

Administrators and Instructional Coaches will analyze classroom progress monitoring data (Eureka Math Equip, Pear Assessment) and adjust instruction and groupings accordingly. Interim ATLAS assessment data will be analyzed each administration and instruction adjusted accordingly. Math Intervention Plans will be reviewed and updated each nine weeks based on student progress data. Spring 2027 ATLAS Summative data will be analyzed to determine whether proficiency targets (49% in 3rd grade; 60% in 4th grade) were met. Data will be shared with the district math coalition and ILT as part of the structured plan review process.
