# Action Plan 2024/2025

<u>Area of Concern</u> The outcomes of the 2022/23 ACT Aspire assessment shed light on a notable concern within Conway Public Schools, particularly regarding English Language Arts (ELA). Analysis of the data indicated that only 54.84% of eighth-grade students and 59.50% of ninth-grade students achieved readiness or exceeded expectations in ELA. This indicates a significant portion of students may require additional support and resources to enhance their proficiency in this subject area. Recognizing the importance of strong ELA skills for overall academic success, Conway Public Schools is poised to address this challenge through targeted interventions and initiatives aimed at bolstering student achievement in English Language Arts.

<u>Goal</u>

- 60% of CJHS 8th grade students will score ready or exceeding on the 2023 ELA Atlas test.
- 65% of CJHS 9th grade students will score ready or exceeding on the 2023 ELA Atlas test.
<u>Strategies</u>

a. ELA teachers will provide explicit teaching of reading strategies for literary and informational text that utilize the notice and note protocol for close reading outlined by Kylene Beers and Robert Probst.
i. All teachers of social studies will provide explicit teaching of reading strategies for nonfiction.
b. Core teachers will provide instruction based on Marzano’s research for direct instruction, using strategies for chunking content, processing content, and allowing students to record and represent their knowledge of the content.

c. ELA teachers will provide explicit teaching of a three-stage instructional model: first, open with an activity and/or prompt for engaging students and building topic knowledge; second, analyze and apply a clear and effective design that engages students as they develop standards-based skills; third, collaborate and compare with peers utilizing multiple text selections with annotation and comparative tasks.
d. ELA teachers will instruct students in the use of a CPSD standardized annotation method to be shared and implemented cross-curricularly district-wide.
e. Core teachers will implement explicit teaching of cross-curricular vocabulary through Greek and Latin roots and affixes.
f. ELA teachers will provide explicit instruction of grade-level grammar content with a focus on both function and form.
g. ELA teachers will provide students with regular writing assignments for developing explicitly taught grammar skills and provide individual and unique feedback to each student. CJHS will maintain cross-curricular expectations for writing well-developed paragraphs with proper punctuation, capitalization, and grammar.
i. Math teachers provide instruction on and opportunities for students to write constructed responses using a three-step model: (1) break down a word problem, (2) annotate the word problem, and (3) underline key words in order to solve the problem.
h. ELA teachers will implement unit plans with a common structure and pacing for all grade levels of ELA which includes morphology, grammar, paragraph writing, one-on-one writers’ conferences, feedback/revision, signpost/questioning reading strategies, and close reading strategies for standards. <u>Professional Development</u> Core teachers received professional training in evidence-based strategies for reading comprehension to equip them to meet the diverse needs of their students during the 2023 school year and will remediate during the 2024 school year.

Core teachers received professional development in evidence-based strategies for direct instruction (chunking content, processing content, and recording and representing content) during the summer of 2023.

ELA teachers will participate in the development of a content and curriculum guide based on the scope and sequence of the HMH intoLiterature textbook.

<u>Implementation Plan</u>

- Spring 2019, CPSD provided professional development training for core teachers, and teachers were provided time to build reading strategies into their unit plans.
- Summer 2021, CPSD provided professional development for instructional strategies for direct instruction based on Robert Marzano’s The New Art and Science of Teaching, and teachers were provided time to build these instructional strategies into their unit plans.
- ELA teachers will combine previous training of literacy based strategies and the 3-stage instructional model to practice in classroom instruction.
- ELA teachers will continue to implement literacy strategies in reading fundamentals, reading literature, and reading informational text using essential questions to achieve key learning objectives.
- ELA teachers will incorporate constructed response items into their lessons as well as assessments in each unit.
- Walkthroughs and classroom assessment data will be used to assess implementation of these strategies.
- ELA teachers will collaborate and analyze data during the PLC process to monitor student progress, achievement, pacing, and reteaching. ■ Teachers will create data walls for all common assessments. ■ Teachers will reassess following a five to ten day reteach window.
- Summer 2024, CPSD provided professional development for instructional strategies for direct instruction and proficiency scales, Learning Ladders, based on Robert Marzano’s The New Art and Science of Teaching, and teachers were provided time to build these instructional strategies into their unit plans.
- ELA teachers will continue to utilize high-yield strategies from Marzano’s The New Art and Science of Teaching.
- Walkthroughs will be used to assess implementation of these strategies.
<u>Evaluation</u> Common formative assessment data and Atlas summative data will be utilized to determine the ongoing effectiveness of the curriculum.

## Conway Junior High School School Action Plan 2024/25

<u>Area Of Concern</u> The findings from the 2023 ACT Aspire assessment revealed math as a prominent area of concern within Conway Public Schools. Analysis of the data uncovered that only 49.61% of eighth-grade students and 41.21% of ninth-grade students achieved readiness or exceeded expectations in math. These statistics underscore a significant portion of students who may require additional support and resources to improve their proficiency in mathematics. Acknowledging the pivotal role of mathematical skills in academic success, Conway Public Schools is committed to implementing targeted interventions and initiatives to address this challenge and enhance student achievement in math.

<u>Goal</u>

- 60% of CJHS 8th grade students will score ready or exceeding in math on the 2024 ATLAS assessment.
- 51% of CJHS 9th grade students will score proficient or above in math on the 2024 ATLAS assessment.
<u>Strategies</u>

- Implementation of EnVision, a vertically aligned instructional textbook, as the anchor text for grades eight through twelve in the 2024/25 school year
- Utilization of Marzano's research-based strategies for direct instruction by math teachers
- Strategies include chunking content, processing content, and utilizing proficiency scales
- Provision of explicit teaching and practice opportunities for students to write constructed responses by math teachers
- Three-step model:
- Writing a general statement on problem-solving approach
- Showing and/or explaining specific problem-solving work
- Writing a conclusion statement with the answer
- Integration of explicit teaching of math vocabulary using Greek and Latin roots and affixes (morphology) by math teachers

<u>Professional Development</u>

- Math teachers received professional development in evidence-based strategies for direct instruction (proficiency scales, chunking content, processing content, and recording and representing content) during the summer curriculum and instruction professional development training 2024.
- Teachers not in attendance for the Marzano training in June 2024 will attend alternate training dates prior to the first day of school.
- Math teachers will participate in the development of content and curriculum guide based on the scope and sequence of the EnVision textbook.
- Math teachers will receive professional development during August 2024 in writing constructed response items using a 3-step model. (1) Write a general statement on how the problem is to be solved, (2) show and/or explain their work in solving the specific problem, and (3) write a conclusion statement with the answer to the problem.
- Teachers will be given time to provide feedback and find or create constructed response items for each unit.
<u>Implementation Plan</u>

- Math teachers will combine previous training of literacy based strategies and the 3-step constructed response model to practice in classroom instruction.
- Math teachers will continue to implement literacy strategies in math using vocabulary/morphology and reading/writing constructed response items.
- Math teachers will incorporate constructed response items into their lessons as well as assessments in each unit.
- Walkthroughs and classroom assessment data will be used to assess implementation of these strategies.
- Math teachers collaborate and analyze data during the PLC process to monitor student progress, achievement, pacing, and reteaching. ■ Teachers will create data walls for all common assessments. ■ Teachers will re-assess following a five to ten day reteach window.
- Summer 2024, CPSD provided professional development for instructional strategies for direct instruction and proficiency scales, Learning Ladders, based on Robert Marzano’s The New Art and Science of Teaching, and teachers were provided time to build these instructional strategies into their unit plans.

- Math teachers will continue to utilize high-yield strategies from Marzano’s The New Art and Science of Teaching.
- Walkthroughs will be used to assess implementation of these strategies.
<u>Evaluation</u>

- Learning Ladders will be implemented to create accountability and awareness for both students and teachers.
- Common assessment data will be utilized to determine progress, growth, and re-teaching.
- ATLAS interim data and ATLAS summative data will be utilized to determine the ongoing effectiveness of the curriculum.

Conway Junior High School School Action Plan 2024-2025

<u>Area of Concern</u> The presence of both physical and verbal conflicts among students serves as a significant distraction, impeding their ability to fully engage in the learning process. Moreover, such conflicts not only disrupt the academic atmosphere but also contribute to the cultivation of a negative school culture, which can have far-reaching consequences on the overall educational experience. Additionally, when conflicts arise, valuable instructional time is often lost as efforts are redirected towards conflict resolution rather than teaching and learning. Recognizing the gravity of this issue, the collaborative efforts of school administration, dedicated staff members, and concerned parents have been instrumental in identifying and addressing student conflicts as a top priority. By acknowledging the impact of these conflicts and actively working towards their resolution, the school community can strive towards creating a more conducive learning environment where students can thrive academically and socially.

<u>Goal</u> Throughout the upcoming 2024/25 academic year, Central Junior High School (CJHS) is committed to implementing measures aimed at diminishing the occurrence of student conflicts by 10% compared to the levels observed in 2023. By achieving this reduction, CJHS anticipates a corresponding decrease in both in-school and out-of-school suspensions, consequently fostering an environment more conducive to student achievement. This proactive approach towards mitigating conflicts not only enhances the overall school climate but also aligns with the school's broader mission of promoting academic excellence and holistic student development.

<u>Strategies</u>

- Introduction of Act 1084 initiatives at CJHS
- Implementation of an incentive program to promote positive behaviors among students
- Recognition and rewards for exemplary conduct
- Categories such as Cat Chow, Colossal Cats, and Cha Cha Cats to encourage continuous improvement
- Fresh start after each event to enable students to refocus on positive behavior
- Delivery of teacher-led lessons on conflict resolution to equip students with essential skills

- Introduction of a citizenship training program for students involved in conflicts, emphasizing responsible behavior and community participation
- Launch of the "See Something/Say Something" campaign to encourage students to report potential acts of violence or threats on the CJHS campus
<u>Professional Development</u> Teachers will need professional development on conflict resolution lessons. This professional development will occur in August of 2024 and will be facilitated by the CJHS administrative staff and instructional facilitators. CJHS trained two teachers on the citizenship training program during the 2022 school year.

<u>Implementation Plan</u>

- Pam Ferguson, Marquis Rogers, Ben Broyles, & Jennifer Dozler will be responsible for this goal’s implementation and progress monitoring.
- CJHS counselors Deanna Robbins, Shawn Finch, Tina Lampe, & Monica Williams will facilitate the citizenship training program.
- CJHS administration will train students on the conflict resolution program as a whole during the first week of school through an assembly.
- CJHS administration will be responsible for assigning students to citizen training as conflicts occur.
- Progress monitoring will occur through pulling discipline data from eschoolPLUS, citizenship training observations, SmartData, and classroom observations.
<u>Evaluation</u> Goal success will be evaluated at the end of the 2024/25 school year.

- CJHS administration will use discipline data from eSchoolPLUS to determine the change in the number of conflicts during the 2024/25 school year.
- We will monitor and adjust program components as needed based on an evaluation of the program.
