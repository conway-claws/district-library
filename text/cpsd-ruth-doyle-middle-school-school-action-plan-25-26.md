## Ruth Doyle Middle School 2025-2026 School Action Plan

At Ruth Doyle Middle School (RDMS), we are committed to fostering a culture of academic excellence and ensuring that every student experiences meaningful growth. Recognizing the importance of supporting our most at-risk learners, RDMS has established a focused goal to increase the percentage of students in the lowest 25% meeting their target growth in English Language Arts from 56% to 67% by May 2026, as measured by the ATLAS assessment. In addition, RDMS aims to continue building a positive school culture by reducing behavior incidents even further and strengthening our school community. This plan outlines the comprehensive strategies, professional development initiatives, and instructional supports that will guide our efforts to close achievement gaps, promote literacy across all content areas, reduce behavior incidents, and empower every student to reach their full potential.

## <u>Goal #1</u>

By May 2026, RDMS will increase the percentage of students in the lowest 25% of performers meeting their target growth in English Language Arts on the ATLAS assessment from 56% to 67%.

## <u>Action Steps for Goal #1</u>

**Teachers** will utilize available data (24/25 Spring ATLAS, 25/26 Fall and Spring ATLAS Interim Assessments, and Common Formative Assessments (CFAs)) during scheduled collaborative Professional Learning Communities (PLCs) to identify students not meeting grade-level standards, reflect on instructional practices, and plan targeted interventions. Interventions will use state- and district-approved High-Quality Instructional Materials (HQIM) and evidence-based reading instruction aligned with the Science of Reading (SoR). **RDMS administrators and instructional coaches** will collect, distribute, and guide teachers through analysis of assessment data during collaborative PLCs and professional development workdays. This data will be used to identify student growth trends and plan intervention strategies.

**Teachers** will monitor academic growth using standards-based, district-developed proficiency scales (Learning Ladders), CFAs, and ATLAS Interim Assessment data to adjust instruction as needed. **The RDMS Library Media Specialist** will implement the Beanstack program to encourage and build a culture of reading by incentivizing and recognizing independent reading for all students. **RDMS counselors** will use 24/25 ATLAS performance reports to ensure that incoming fifth-grade students in the lowest 25% are scheduled into additional ELA instruction groups ("ELA skill will") early in the school year. **Teachers** will participate in curriculum and instruction professional development offered by Conway Public Schools and/or the Arch Ford Education Service Cooperative. This PD will support the implementation of HQIM and SoR-aligned practices. **All content area teachers** will receive professional development and support to integrate evidence-based reading strategies into their classrooms, further building a schoolwide culture of literacy.

## <u>Professional Development and Support for Goal #1</u>

**Teachers** will attend summer professional development offered by Conway Public Schools and the Arch Ford Education Service Cooperative to strengthen their instructional practices and deepen their understanding of HQIM and SoR-aligned strategies. **Teachers** will continue to participate in collaborative PLCs throughout the school year with content- and grade-level-alike peers. These PLCs will focus on analyzing student performance data, identifying levels of proficiency, and planning targeted instruction and interventions. **Teachers** will continue to engage in collaborative PLCs throughout the school year to receive guidance and support on deepening their understanding of how to interpret ATLAS Interim data, CFAs, and learning ladders. **Instructional coaches** will provide ongoing support for vertical and horizontal alignment of grade-level content. They will work with teachers to strengthen the use of student Learning Ladders, HQIM, and intervention and enrichment strategies during What I Need (WIN) Time and in regular classroom instruction. **Instructional coaches** will provide training and support for all subject area teachers (math, science, social studies, electives) on incorporating literacy strategies like: close reading of complex texts, vocabulary development, and writing to explain thinking. This will build on strategies to support struggling readers in non-ELA classrooms.

## <u>Goal #2</u>

By May 2026, RDMS will continue to strengthen its positive school culture by maintaining and further reducing behavior incidents by an additional 10%, as measured by office discipline referrals (ODRs). This will be achieved through the ongoing implementation of classroom behavior social contracts, targeted WIN Time sessions focused on enrichment, intervention, and behavior support, and the

continuation of grade-level incentive points, events, and rewards to reinforce positive behaviors.

## <u>Action Steps for Goal #2</u>

**Teachers** will create and implement classroom social contracts at the beginning of the year. **Teachers** will continue to reinforce the social contracts throughout the year. **Teachers** will model and practice positive behaviors regularly, holding students accountable to the terms of their social contracts. **Administrators** will provide ongoing support to teachers in managing and adjusting social contracts as needed. **Counselors and Administrators** track discipline referrals and will conduct small-group sessions within WIN Time to address social-emotional learning, conflict resolution, and problem-solving strategies. **Administrators and counselors** will establish a clear system of earning and tracking grade-level incentive points (e.g., tone, lines, cleanliness TLC). **Administrators** will host informational sessions for students and families at the beginning of the year to explain the school’s behavior expectations, rewards, and consequences. **Administrators, teachers, and counselors** will provide ongoing support for at-risk students by working together to identify students exhibiting chronic behavior challenges and offer individualized interventions such as counseling check-ins or individualized incentive structures. **Students** will be regularly acknowledged for their positive behavior through assemblies, shout-outs, or being selected as student of the month. **Administrators** will organize monthly character assemblies for students, featuring guest speakers from the community to explain the importance of the featured character trait of that month. **Administrators, counselors, and district social worker:** At-Risk Students: Utilize SRT Team meetings to review data on students receiving individualized behavior interventions. Track progress and note whether behavior incidents decrease for these students as a result of additional support. Also, for students with chronic behavior issues, assess the effectiveness of interventions like check-in/check-out systems, counseling, or mentoring.

## <u>Professional Development and Support for Goal #2</u>

**Teachers** will receive training and support on constructing proactive classroom social contracts, the RDMS three-tiered classroom behavior system, parental contact/involvement, office referral protocols, and behavior data analysis. **Teachers** will also receive PD on how to celebrate and recognize positive behavior and student success within the classroom, reinforcing the importance of a positive and motivating environment for both students and teachers. **Administrators and instructional coaches** will provide targeted support throughout the year, including implementing behavioral strategies and

maintaining social contracts. Behavior data will be monitored and communicated to teachers, where teachers can collaboratively review the data, share insights, and adjust strategies based on trends. **Administrators, counselors, and the district’s behavioral specialist** will work with teachers who need extra support managing specific behavior challenges, providing real-time advice and intervention strategies.
