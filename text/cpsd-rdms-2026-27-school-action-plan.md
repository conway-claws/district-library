## Ruth Doyle Middle School 2026-2027 School Action Plan

At Ruth Doyle Middle School (RDMS), we are dedicated to an ongoing cycle of refinement to ensure our culture of academic excellence translates into measurable student success. While we have established a strong foundation, we are committed to sharpening our data protocols and intervention strategies to move the percentage of our lowest 25% of learners meeting their ELA growth targets from 52% to 56% proficiency by May 2027. Simultaneously, we are evolving our behavioral frameworks to proactively reduce incidents and deepen our sense of community and student belonging. This plan details how we will continuously evaluate and enhance our instructional supports and professional development to more effectively close achievement gaps and empower every student.

## Goal #1

**Goal #1:** By May 2027, RDMS will increase the percentage of students meeting their target growth in English Language Arts on the ATLAS summative assessment from 56% to 60%. This growth will be driven by a specialized focus on reducing "Below Mastery" percentages in Reading Fundamentals and Reading Information through the systematic implementation of Tier II small group interventions and Tier III Orton-Gillingham (OG) based reading programs.

## <u>Action Steps for Goal #1</u>

Building Intervention Capacity: RDMS will continue training reading interventionists to deliver Tier II and Tier III OG-based programs, such as Take Flight, Rite Flight, and Sonday Systems, specifically targeting students who demonstrate a need for these interventions. Data-Driven Scheduling: RDMS counselors will use 25/26 ATLAS performance reports to ensure the lowest 25% of performers and those identified with foundational gaps are scheduled into "ELA skill" groups for 5th and 6th-grade and OG interventions early in the 2026-2027 school year. Teachers will leverage real-time assessment data to organize fluid Tier II small groups, providing concentrated support to students in the bottom quartile to accelerate their mastery of foundational skills.

PLC Data Protocols: Instructional coaches and teachers will refine and continue to implement Learning Ladders (proficiency scales), Common Formative Assessments (CFAs), and Interim data during weekly PLCs to identify students for intervention and reflect on the efficacy of SoR-aligned practices. RDMS will refine the 5th and 6th-grade intervention schedule into a high-fidelity guided model, where student placement in enrichment or intervention tiers is continuously optimized through the analysis of current performance data. Classroom Integration: All content area teachers (Math, Science, Social Studies) will receive PD on incorporating literacy strategies—such as close reading of complex texts and vocabulary development—to support struggling readers outside the ELA classroom. Culture of Reading: The Library Media Specialist will continue the Beanstack program to incentivize independent reading and build a schoolwide culture of literacy.

## Professional Development and Support for Goal #1

Teachers will attend summer professional development offered by Conway Public Schools and the Arch Ford Education Service Cooperative to strengthen their instructional practices and deepen their understanding of HQIM and SoR-aligned strategies. Teachers will continue to participate in and refine collaborative PLCs throughout the school year with content- and grade-level-alike peers. These PLCs will continue to increase the focus on analyzing student performance data, identifying levels of proficiency, and planning targeted instruction and interventions. Teachers will continue to engage in collaborative PLCs throughout the school year to receive guidance and support on deepening their understanding of how to interpret ATLAS Interim data, CFAs, and learning ladders. Instructional coaches will provide ongoing support for vertical and horizontal alignment of grade-level content. They will work with teachers to strengthen the use of student Learning Ladders, HQIM, and intervention and enrichment strategies during What I Need (WIN) Time and in regular classroom instruction. Instructional coaches will provide training and support for all subject area teachers (math, science, social studies, electives) on incorporating literacy strategies like: close reading of complex texts, vocabulary development, and writing to explain thinking. This will build on strategies to support struggling readers in non-ELA classrooms.

## <u>Goal #2</u>

By May 2027, RDMS will strengthen its school culture by reducing behavior incidents by 10-15% compared to the 2025-2026 baseline. Recognizing an increase in classroom-based referrals, the school will increase consistency and enforcement over time of classroom social contracts and utilizing WIN Time for targeted social-emotional intervention and restorative discipline practices.

## Action Steps for Goal #2

**Social Contracts:** Teachers will create, implement, and reinforce classroom social contracts at the start of the year, modeling positive behaviors and holding students accountable to these agreed-upon terms. **Administrators** will provide ongoing support to teachers in managing and adjusting social contracts as needed. **Increasing Tier II Behavioral Strategies:** Administrators and counselors will increase the frequency of Tier II behavior interventions. Counselors and Administrators will track discipline referrals and conduct small-group sessions during WIN Time focusing on social-emotional learning (SEL), conflict resolution, and problem-solving. **Administrators and counselors** will establish a tracking system for grade-level incentive points that prioritizes targeted positive behaviors, specifically rewarding students for demonstrating conflict resolution, active engagement, and adherence to social contracts. By shifting the focus from monitoring compliance to incentivizing 'pro-social' actions, RDMS aims to directly counteract the recent increase in ODRs.

**Administrators** will host informational sessions for students and families at the beginning of the year to explain the school’s behavior expectations, rewards, and consequences. **Administrators, teachers, and counselors** will provide ongoing support for at-risk students by working together to identify students exhibiting chronic behavior challenges and offer individualized interventions such as counseling check-ins or individualized incentive structures. **Students** will be regularly acknowledged for their positive behavior through assemblies, shout-outs, or being selected as student of the month. **Administrators, counselors, and district social worker:** At-Risk Students: Utilize SRT Team meetings to review data on students receiving individualized behavior interventions. Track progress and note whether behavior incidents decrease for these students as a result of additional support. Also, for students with chronic behavior issues, assess the effectiveness of interventions like check-in/check-out systems, counseling, or mentoring.

## Professional Development and Support for Goal #2

**Teachers** will receive training and support on constructing proactive classroom social contracts, the RDMS three-tiered classroom behavior system, parental contact/involvement, office referral protocols, and behavior data analysis. **Teachers** will also receive PD on how to celebrate and recognize positive behavior and student success within the classroom, reinforcing the importance of a positive and motivating environment for both students and teachers. **Administrators and instructional coaches** will provide targeted support throughout the year, including implementing behavioral strategies and maintaining social contracts. Behavior data will be monitored and communicated to teachers, where teachers can collaboratively review the data, share insights, and adjust strategies based on trends. **Administrators, counselors, and the district’s behavioral specialist** will work with teachers who need extra support managing specific behavior challenges, providing real-time advice and intervention strategies.
