Anthony is marking his 11th year as a teacher and coach, currently in his 2nd year as the head baseball coach at Gentry High School. His coaching career has also included 6 years as the head baseball/head golf coach at Ouachita High School, a year as assistant baseball/head tennis coach at Sheridan High School, and 2 years as assistant baseball and 1 year as head golf coach at Marion High School.

CHS Baseball coaches Noel Boucher, Barry Lueders, and Ryan Reed left a lasting impact, shaping his love for the game and life itself. He recalls the excitement of cheering on his classmates at football games and supporting every volleyball and basketball game during his high school years.

Anthony’s favorite teacher was Jim Owen, whose dynamic approach to teaching history made it feel alive and engaging. Jim’s memorable “Owenisms” and his lesson on using our “B.S. detectors” are still relevant today, especially in an age of misinformation.

Advice for Current Students:
“Enjoy your time as a Wampus Cat—the years go by faster than you think. The friendships you make from kindergarten through 12th grade are some of the best you’ll ever have, so make an effort to stay in touch after graduation.”

Update Message: Sent 09-20-24

Conway Public School Community, 

Please read both of these important messages regarding School Safety information related to Conway Public Schools. Please know that we continue to make the safety of our students and staff the number one priority. 

We understand there has been some confusion surrounding recent articles about threats involving Carl Stuart Middle School. Please know that the information being circulated relates to an incident that occurred three years ago. It is unrelated to the recent situation affecting our district.

The email sent to parents and staff by Conway Public Schools referred to an anonymous, statewide threat that has been circulating for the past week, which has reached some students in our district. The message did not target any specific school or district, and we want to assure you that there have been no direct threats made against Carl Stuart Middle School or any other school in Conway Public Schools at this time.

While there is no specific threat to our schools at this time, we are taking all necessary precautions and working closely with the Conway Police Department to monitor the situation. Below is the message that was sent to parents and staff on 09-19-24:

Email Message sent 09-19-24
Dear Conway Public Schools,

We want to make you aware of a situation impacting several school districts across Arkansas. Over the past few weeks, an anonymous threatening message has been circulating, unfortunately reaching students in our district. As always, Conway Public Schools takes any and all threats seriously, and we are handling this matter with the utmost care and attention.

Our district remains highly vigilant, and will continue working closely with local law enforcement to ensure the safety of our students and staff. We want to remind everyone that threats made against schools are treated with the highest level of seriousness. Any students found responsible for such threats and/or sharing these types of messages will face school-based disciplinary action and legal consequences.

We encourage you to take a moment to discuss with your student the serious consequences of making or sharing harmful threats. These actions cause alarm and disrupt the learning environment. Please remind your student to immediately inform school personnel if they come across any concerning messages or situations.

Thank you for your cooperation and commitment to keeping our schools safe. As always, please feel free to contact CPSD with any questions, concerns, or information.

Sincerely,
Conway Public Schools

### 2024-12-19 · Conway Public Schools (id 7346457)

🎭 Throwback Thursday: Honoring Mr. James H. Clark 🎭

Today, we’re celebrating the legacy of Mr. James H. Clark, a true cornerstone of Conway Public Schools. Associated with CHS since his graduation in 1948, Mr. Clark joined the faculty in 1955 as a coach and went on to dedicate 42 years to public education, with 33 of those years spent shaping the future at CPSD. His impact is so profound that the CHS auditorium proudly bears his name, ensuring his legacy continues to inspire generations.
