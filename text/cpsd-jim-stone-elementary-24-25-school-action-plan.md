## Jim Stone Elementary School School Action Plan 2024-2025

## Area of Concern

An area of concern for Jim Stone Elementary is our student’s ability to read at grade level. Research shows that reading is not only critical to a child’s success in school, but also their lifelong earnings and their ability to eventually contribute to the nation’s economy. Additionally, the LEARNS Act states that students who do not meet the third-grade reading standard by the 2024-2025 school year will not be promoted to the fourth grade. With this information and the review of our 2023-2024 NWEA and ACT Aspire data our leadership team identified reading as an area of concern.

Students reading at grade level is an area of concern which grows out of the understanding that the ability to read is critical to a child's success in school, lifelong earnings, and their ability to contribute to the nation's economy. Reading at grade level by the third grade is an indicator as to if a child will graduate. Therefore, the teachers and the school leadership team identified reading as an area of concern based on the 2023-24 NWEA and ACT Aspire data.

## <u>Goal</u>

The leadership team discussed the importance of increasing reading performance and the mastery of core foundational skills in the early grades in order to increase overall achievement of students in third and fourth grade.

- Each grade level will work to increase the overall percentage of students performing at or above the district grade level Mean RIT by 5% on the Spring NWEA.
- By the end of the school year, 60% of the students in 3rd and 4th grade will be performing at or above grade level in reading based on scores from ATLAS.
## <u>Strategies</u>

- Implement 95% and Benchmark HQIMs with fidelity
- Provide a daily Tier II intervention group to students performing below grade level based on individual needs
- Utilize Lexia to reinforce literacy skills
- Utilize the states new K-2 Assessment and K-3 Screener to monitor data to further guide instruction AND monitor student progress
- Provide teachers training on the new state assessments and screeners
- Assess students’ learning frequently; Provide timely reports of formative assessments to be used in PLCs
- Assess learning with common grade level assessments and plan small group instruction based on student’s abilities
- Increase feedback to students on literacy assessments
- Observe other teachers in the building and at other schools implementing the HQIMs
## <u>Professional Development</u>

This summer the K-4 grade teachers will receive opportunities to attend HQIM training sessions.

- Professional development on the use of data will be addressed throughout the year. (How to analyze and then use the information to create targeted interventions)
- During PLC meetings, teachers will analyze data to collectively determine the steps necessary to ensure all students are making adequate progress
- Provide teachers with opportunities to attend Benchmark and 95% trainings through Arch Ford

## <u>Evaluation</u>

Formal implementation will occur in the Fall of 2024 and grade levels will be provided ongoing professional development throughout the year. Evaluation will begin the first months of school and continue throughout the year. This formative evaluation will include the following:

- Progress Monitoring
- Classroom walk-throughs and observations of 95% and Benchmark
- Review results of classroom assessment data and make decisions about instructional needs
- Identify students in need of intervention and provide the necessary interventions
- Monitoring progress through the states new K-2 Assessment

## Jim Stone Elementary School School Action Plan 2024-2025

## Area of Concern

For the 2023-24 school year, Jim Stone Elementary saw an increase in overall behavior referrals; however, knowing that behavior directly impacts learning, Jim Stone will continue to focus on the improvement of student behavior. Overall student behavior referrals from Kindergarten through Fourth grade are as follows:

|Subgroup|Male|Female|# of Students|
|---|---|---|---|
|Black or African American|35|2|37|
|Hispanic or Latino|5|0|5|
|Two or More Races|1|1|2|
|White|35|1|36|
|TOTAL|76|4|80|

## Goal

Our goal is to decrease not only the number of office referrals but also the number of behavior incidents all together by 10%. The leadership team noticed that several of the referrals were on the same student multiple times. Therefore, the team chose to focus on building positive relationships with all students in the building.

## Strategies

- Implement a committee of teachers and staff that will assist overseeing positive behavior strategies school wide.
- Utilize positive office referrals

- Ensure quality time is spent on classroom routines and procedures
- Utilize the Benchmark Launch teacher guide to ensure Benchmark HQIM routines and procedures are taught school wide
- Implement a school wide Morning Meeting time
- Build a culture of caring
- Implement a school wide behavior system and rewards
## <u>Professional Development</u>

Kindergarten through Fourth Grade teachers will receive positive behavior training at Jim Stone through the Mighty Stallion Committee. This training is designed to increase teacher focus on building positive relationships with all students. We will continue throughout the year to include behavior support in our PLCs, as well as, peer coaching or other methods.

## <u>Evaluation</u>

Ongoing analysis of behavior referrals will be conducted throughout the school year. The team will continuously monitor the data to ensure interventions are in place for students receiving multiple behavior referrals in order to decrease the frequency and/or severity.

- Run a report from eSchool to monitor the number of referrals each month–look for patterns
- Run a report from eSchool to identify and either intensify or change interventions being implemented for students with multiple referrals

- Monitor the number of positive office referrals and continuously promote positive referrals to staff.
