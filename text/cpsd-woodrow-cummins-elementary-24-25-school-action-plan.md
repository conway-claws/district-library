## <u>Area of Concern</u>

Third grade reading level has been shown as a predictor of graduation and college attendance, even when demographic characteristics were included as controls (Chapin Hill, 2010). Only 31% of Arkansas’ 4th-grade students are proficient in reading. Arkansas ranks in the lower third in reading scores in comparison to other states. Failure to read by 9 years of age portends a lifetime of illiteracy for at least 70 percent of struggling readers (Shaywitz, 2003).

Teachers and administrators identified reading at grade level as a critical component of student achievement and success. Data from the 2018, 2019, (no testing in 2020 due to COVID) 2021, 2022, & 2023 ACT Aspire testing sessions supports the need for increased reading achievement, as shown below:

- Spring 2018 ACT Aspire Data, 64% of third and fourth grade students were reading at or above grade level. Achievement data is as follows:
- 221 third & fourth grade students were included in the testing group ■ 143 students scored “Ready” or “Exceeding” ■ 78 students (36%) scored “In Need of Support” or “Close”
- Spring 2019 ACT Aspire Data, 64% of third and fourth grade students were reading at or above grade level. Achievement data is as follows:
- 186 third & fourth grade students were included in the testing group ■ 119 students scored “Ready” or “Exceeding” ■ 67 students (36%) scored “In Need of Support” or “Close”
- Spring 2021 ACT Aspire Data, 54% of third and fourth grade students were reading at or above grade level. Achievement data is as follows:
- 202 third & fourth grade students were included in the testing group ■ 110 students scored “Ready” or “Exceeding” ■ 92 students (46%) scored “In Need of Support” or “Close”
- Spring 2022 ACT Aspire Data, 51% of third and fourth grade students were reading at or above grade level. Achievement data is as follows:
- 180 third & fourth grade students were included in the testing group ■ 92 students scored “Ready” or “Exceeding” ■ 88 students scored “In Need of Support” or “Close”
- Spring 2023 ACT Aspire Data, 61% of third and fourth grade students were reading at or above grade level. Achievement data is as follows:
- 187 third & fourth grade students were included in the testing group ■ 114 students scored “Ready” or “Exceeding” ■ 73 students scored “In Need of Support” or “Close”

## Goal

Our school goal is to increase current reading achievement by focusing on the number of students not reading at or above grade level. Based on the 2023 ACT Aspire data, our goal is to increase the number of third and fourth grade students scoring “Ready” or “Exceeding” from our current reality of 61% to 70%.

Our focus will include students at all grade levels, not just the third and fourth graders. Research shows that children who enter kindergarten, at risk of reading failure, can learn to read at average or above-average levels—if they are identified early and given systematic, explicit instruction in phonemic awareness, phonics, reading fluency, vocabulary, and reading comprehension strategies (Lyon et al., 2001; Torgesen, 2002a). In an effort to close the achievement gap with struggling readers, we will use NWEA data (in the chart below) to track long term growth of students in kindergarten through second grade.

## Strategies

- The focus for below grade level readers will begin with becoming proficient at word recognition (phonological awareness, decoding, and sight word fluency).
- Language comprehension (background knowledge, vocabulary, language structures, verbal reasoning, and literacy knowledge) will largely be taught through oral instruction until students are proficient in word recognition.
- Once students form automaticity in word recognition, the focus will shift to building language comprehension through guided and independent reading.
- Decodable readers will be used to build automaticity.

- Kindergarten through fourth grade teachers will use Benchmark Reading Workshop for Tier I reading instruction to build language comprehension strategies.
- Kindergarten through fourth grade teachers will implement 95% Group Core Program for Tier I phonics instruction.
- Tier II supplemental instruction will be provided to students performing below grade level.
- If students are not making adequate academic growth progressing they will be brought to the RTI committee to discuss additional academic support.
- Instructional Facilitator will curriculum plan with grade levels and model effective lessons and strategies using HQIM.
- Activity teachers will push in the classrooms to assist with small group intervention.
- Incentives will be offered to students based on targeted growth.

## <u>Evaluation</u>

Empirical research clearly indicates the urgent need to provide high quality, intensive instructional interventions to students at risk of reading difficulty as soon as possible (Snow, Burns, and Griffin, 1998). Woodrow Cummmins will administer screening measures to every student in kindergarten through fourth grade in an effort to identify students at risk for reading difficulties. Interventions will be implemented for identified students based on assessment data and current class achievement. Response to Intervention (RTI) meetings will convene to assess current intervention practices and/or develop strategies needed to ensure student success. Administrators and Instructional Facilitator will conduct classroom walkthroughs and provide feedback.

## <u>Area of Concern</u>

It is the responsibility of Conway Public Schools to ensure the safety of all students and school personnel. Safe, effective, evidence-based strategies should be the basis for protocols at Woodrow Cummins Elementary to support every student who displays challenging behavior at school. Providing school personnel with training that is focused on evidence-based positive behavior support and de-escalation techniques can reduce the incidence of trauma. The effective implementation of school-wide behavioral support is linked to greater academic achievement, significantly fewer disciplinary problems, increased instruction time, and the perception of a safer teaching environment by school personnel. Positive behavioral support involves school-wide approaches that result in:

a) Positive classroom and school climates;
b) Prosocial student and school personnel interactions;
c) Teaching a student academic, social, emotional, behavioral engagement, and achievement skills; and
d) Reinforcing the academic, social, emotional, behavioral engagement, and achievement skills of a student;
## Goal

Woodrow Cummins Elementary will provide social-emotional support to students in a variety of ways.Through our Positive Behavior Support System Team, school personnel will be trained on the Three-Tiered Behavior Response to Intervention (RTI) Process. The counselor will collaborate with classroom teachers to identify specific social-emotional issues that could be addressed in a mulititude of settings such as the classroom, small group, and individually. ## <u>Evaluation</u>

Woodrow Cummmins has established a Positive Behavior System Team that includes school administrators, Counselor, and designated teacher leaders. Data from parent communication, teacher feedback, and positive behavior reports cumulatively help determine student needs for Tier I Intervention.Tier II interventions will be implemented for identified students who are not responding from a social-emotional and behavioral perspective through Tier I Interventions. Teachers will utilize Teacher Access Center (TAC) to submit student misbehavior referrals. Data collected from TAC will be utilized to identify students whose behavior needs will be escalated to the prevention level, which includes: parent/teacher conference, administrator conference, and/or outside counseling. Tier III interventions will be implemented for students who are not responding to Tier II prevention measures. Tier III interventions may include counseling observations, administrative conferences, additional parent-teacher conferences, functional behavioral assessment, etc. Response to Intervention (RTI) meetings will convene to assess current intervention practices and/or develop strategies needed to ensure student success. Administrators, Counselor, and Instructional Facilitator will conduct classroom walkthroughs and provide feedback.
