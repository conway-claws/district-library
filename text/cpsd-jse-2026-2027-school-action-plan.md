## 2026-2027 School Action Plan Jim Stone Elementary School

## <u>Area of Concern: Literacy</u>

Literacy is one key area of need at Jim Stone Elementary. During the 2025-2026 school year, student achievement data reveal the following: 30% of third grade students scored at levels 3 or 4 in ELA (reading and writing) on the ATLAS Interim I assessment; 39% of fourth grade students scored at levels 3 or 4 in ELA (reading and writing) on the ATLAS Interim I assessment; 44% of of third grade students scored at levels 3 or 4 in ELA (reading and writing) on the ATLAS Interim II assessment; 57% of fourth grade students scored at levels 3 or 4 in ELA (reading and writing) on the ATLAS Interim II assessment. During the 2025-2026 school year, student achievement data from the administration of the middle of year DIBELS 8 assessment reveal 51% of kindergarten students scored at or above benchmark, 55% of first grade students scored at or above benchmark, 66% of second grade students scored at or above benchmark, and 70% of third grade students scored at or above benchmark. Additionally, the district collected input through community workshops, a public survey, and a series of internal stakeholder meetings. This feedback was carefully analyzed to identify key themes representing the most frequently expressed needs, challenges, and ideas, which in turn helped shape the district’s strategic plan. The action plan at Jim Stone Elementary School is aligned with and supports the priorities outlined in the district’s strategic plan.

## <u>Goal</u>

The staff members at Jim Stone Elementary will implement effective core instruction and interventions aligned to the Science of Reading (SoR) utilizing high-quality instructional materials (HQIM) with integrity to increase the number of students performing at or above grade-level proficiency standards by 3% as evidenced by summative, interim, and formative assessments by the end of the 2026-2027 school year.

## <u>Strategies/Action Steps</u>

The principal and one fourth grade teacher will participate in district-level Special Ops planning and professional development sessions aligned to the SoR and the implementation of district-adopted HQIMs including Arts and Letters and 95% Group Phonics. K-4 teachers will attend professional development focusing on how to implement the state-approved, district-adopted *Arts and Letters* HQIM core curriculum. Speciality teachers will participate in annual training focusing on the *science of reading.* The reading interventionist will participate in ongoing Take Flight training for dyslexia intervention. The school leadership team, composed of the principal, assistant principal, instructional coach, and counselor, will develop a master schedule for each grade level so that students have dedicated and protected instructional time for core reading instruction and reading interventions.

At the beginning of the 2026-2027 school year, trained teachers will administer the state ATLAS reading screener to all students in grades K-3. Utilizing these data points, coupled with summative assessment data from the Spring of 2026, the leadership team will engage teachers and staff in analyzing the data to determine students who are struggling in reading (including those performing in the lowest quartile) and may need additional testing (such as LevelI or Level II dyslexia screening), develop Individual Reading Plans (IRPs), and form Tier II and Tier III interventions appropriate to each student’s needs. The instructional coach will schedule regular collaborative meetings where teachers work together to internalize lessons using high-quality instructional materials, including *Arts and* *Letters* and *95% Group Core Phonics.* Administrators will conduct classroom walkthroughs as well as formal and informal observations to observe core reading instruction and interventions. From the data collected, administrators will provide feedback to teachers to inform future instruction. Teachers and relevant staff members will hold regular RTI meetings to collaboratively analyze student data and develop, implement, or adjust targeted interventions based on individual student needs, ensuring timely and effective support for academic growth. Teachers and relevant staff members will implement Individual Reading Plans (IRPs) providing targeted instruction and interventions, including *95% Group Core Phonics, Arts and Letters*, *BUILD, UFLI, Rite Flight, Lexia, Take Flight,* and *SPIRE*. Teachers and relevant staff members will monitor student progress and update goals on students’ IRPs at regular intervals. Teachers and staff will communicate student progress in reading to parents in various ways, including face-to-face parent conferences, phone calls, interim progress reports, and report cards. Parents/Guardians of students receiving dyslexia intervention will be provided with a *progress monitoring summary of skills for parent report* each quarter. Additionally, the staff will communicate to families that students’ progress can be checked at all times by accessing their child’s IRP in the ATLAS portal. The family and community engagement committee at Jim Stone Elementary will partner with the Faulkner County Library to plan and host a family engagement event to promote literacy and reading outside of school. The media specialist will organize a schoolwide Read-a-Thon designed to promote a love of reading, increase student engagement, and build literacy skills. This event will include goal-setting, progress tracking, and incentives to encourage participation, as well as opportunities for students to share and celebrate their reading achievements. To supplement instruction, students will have access to Lexia, a state-approved digital learning tool that provides personalized learning opportunities aligned to the science of reading. Students will earn incentives for achieving their individualized Lexia goals.

## <u>Area of Concern: Math</u>

Math is another key area of need at Jim Stone Elementary. During the 2025-2026 school year, student achievement data reveal the following: 7% of third grade students scored at levels 3 or 4 in math on the ATLAS Interim I assessment; 18% of fourth grade students scored at levels 3 or 4 in math on the ATLAS Interim I assessment; 22% of of third grade students scored at levels 3 or 4 in math on the ATLAS Interim II assessment; 29% of fourth grade students scored at levels 3 or 4 in math on the ATLAS Interim II assessment. Additionally, the district collected input through community workshops, a public survey, and a series of internal stakeholder meetings. This feedback was carefully analyzed to identify key themes representing the most frequently expressed needs, challenges, and ideas, which in turn helped shape the district’s strategic plan for mathematics. The action plan at Jim Stone Elementary School is aligned with and supports the priorities outlined in the district’s strategic plan for mathematics.

## <u>Goal</u>

The staff members at Jim Stone Elementary will implement effective core instruction using *Eureka* *Math Squared* and aligned interventions with integrity to increase the number of students performing at or above grade-level proficiency standards by 3% as evidenced by summative, interim, and formative assessments by the end of the 2026-2027 school year.

## <u>Strategies/Action Steps</u>

The instructional coach, along with third and fourth grade teachers who teach math, will attend a two-day professional development Math ACATemy hosted in partnership with the University of Arkansas to improve teachers’ content and pedagogical knowledge in the area of mathematics instruction. The school leadership team, composed of the principal, assistant principal, instructional coach, and counselor, will develop a master schedule for each grade level so that students have dedicated and protected instructional time for mathematics instruction. At the beginning of the 2026-2027 school year, K-2 teachers will administer the state ATLAS screener. Utilizing these data points, coupled with summative assessment data from the Spring of 2026, the leadership team will engage all teachers and staff in analyzing the data to identify students who are struggling in mathematics, including those performing in the lowest quartile and develop Math Improvement Plans (MIPs) for students in grades 3 and 4 with aligned interventions aimed at improving student achievement and growth in mathematics. The instructional coach will schedule regular collaborative meetings where teachers work together to internalize lessons using *Eureka Math Squared*. One fourth grade math teacher will continue to implement a program called *Modern* *Classroom Project* which layers with *Eureka Math Squared* and integrates small group instruction, student goal setting, and mastery-based learning. Through this project, the

teacher will receive ongoing professional support and feedback from observations by experts with the *Modern Classroom Project.* Administrators will conduct classroom walkthroughs as well as formal and informal observations to observe mathematics instruction. From the data collected, administrators will provide feedback to teachers to inform future instruction. Teachers and relevant staff members will hold regular RTI meetings to collaboratively analyze student data and develop, implement, or adjust targeted interventions based on individual student needs, ensuring timely and effective support for academic growth. Teachers in grades 3 and 4 will provide instruction and intervention aligned with the goals in each student’s Math Improvement Plan (MIP). Teachers will monitor student progress and update goals on students’ MIPs at regular intervals. Teachers and staff will communicate student progress to parents in various ways, including face-to-face parent conferences, phone calls, interim progress reports, and report cards. Additionally, the staff will communicate to families that students’ progress can be checked at all times by accessing their child’s scores, and MIP (if applicable) in the ATLAS portal. The Family and Community Engagement Committee at Jim Stone Elementary will host a STEAM Night designed to strengthen partnerships between school, families, and the community. This event will provide interactive opportunities for students and their families to engage in and deepen their understanding of Science, Technology, Engineering, the Arts, and Mathematics.
