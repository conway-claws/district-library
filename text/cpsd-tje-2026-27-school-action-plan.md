#### Purpose & Strategic Focus

This School Action Plan establishes Theodore Jones Elementary’s (TJE) two school-wide goals for 2026–2027. Goals were selected based on a systematic analysis of TJE’s 2024–2025 summative ATLAS report card data to identify the specific accountability measures with the greatest gap from the state average and, therefore, the highest leverage for improving TJE’s letter grade.

#### SMART Goal — ELA

TJE will sustain above-state performance on all three ELA accountability measures while increasing the percentage of students converting from **Specific** Level 2 to Levels 3 & 4 (proficient), with targeted support for students in the lowest quartile to maintain their individual growth trajectories.

By spring 2027 ATLAS summative: (1) ELA Achievement increases from 38% to at least 43% (maintaining above-state status). (2) Growth of All Students — ELA remains at or above 59% (state avg: 53%). (3) Growth of **Measurable** Lowest Quarter — ELA increases from 56% to at least 58% (state avg: 52%). Mid-year indicators: Interim I ELA proficiency ≥25% and Interim II ELA proficiency ≥40% at school level.

TJE’s Interim I → II ELA gains (+21 pts in 3rd grade, +9 pts in 4th grade) demonstrate instruction is working. The HQIM transition to a new Tier 1 **Achievable** program in 2026–27, combined with continued Scottish Rite intervention fidelity and coaching cycles, provides the infrastructure to convert Level 2 students to proficiency while protecting bottom-quartile growth.

Required district goal aligned to the CPSD Strategic Reading Plan’s five-year trajectory of raising ELA proficiency. Also directly tied to **Relevant** Arkansas accountability measures: Achievement, Growth of All Students, and Growth of Lowest Quarter in ELA.

Three formal checkpoints: ATLAS Screeners (September 2026), Interim I **Time-Bound** (October 2026), Interim II (February 2027), and ATLAS Summative

#### Strategic Focus: Converting Level A → Level 3 Students

Because TJE already exceeds the state on ELA growth measures, the primary lever for increasing ELA Achievement from 38% to 43% is identifying and accelerating students who are currently at Level 2 (basic understanding) into Level 3 (proficient). These students are close. They have foundational skills but need structured support to cross the proficiency threshold. This is a fundamentally different instructional challenge than supporting Level 1 students, and the action steps below reflect that distinction.

### SCHOOL-WIDE aOAL A: Mathematics — arowth & Achievement

|Math Measure|TJE 2024-25|State Average|
|---|---|---|
|Achievement — Math|34% ⚠️ (-6 pts)|40%|
|Growth All Students — Math|43% ⚠️ (-9 pts)|52%|
|Growth Lowest Quarter — Math|28% 🚨 (-26 pts)|54%|

TJE is below the state average on all three math accountability measures. The most urgent gap is Math Growth of the Lowest Quarter at 28% vs. the state’s 54%. A 26-point deficit that is the single largest accountability gap in the school and the primary driver of the C rating. Closing this gap requires a fundamentally different approach than general math improvement. The bottom 25% of math students need intensive, targeted, individually-monitored intervention.

#### SMART Goal — Math (Growth + Achievement)

TJE will increase math growth and achievement across all three math accountability measures, with the primary focus on dramatically improving **Specific** the percentage of lowest-quartile students meeting their individual math growth targets, the school’s largest single accountability gap at 26 points below the state average.

By spring 2027, ATLAS summative: (1) Math Growth of Lowest Quarter increases from 28% to at least 40% (state avg: 54%). (2) Math Growth of All Students increases from 43% to at least 50% (state avg: 52%). (3) **Measurable** Math Achievement increases from 34% to at least 39% (state avg: 40%). Mid-year: Interim I Math ≥18% proficient; Interim II Math ≥27% proficient at the school level.

TJE demonstrated strong within-year math growth on interims (3rd grade +12 pts, 4th grade +14 pts from Interim I to II in 2025–26). Eureka Math² provides a coherent HQIM framework. The Math Intervention Plan (MIP) system is already required by Arkansas law for Level 1 students. The **Achievable** district’s Uplifting the Lowest Quartile PD series, math coaching cycles, and WIN time structures provide the infrastructure to specifically target bottom-quartile growth. Reaching 40% on the lowest-quarter growth (from 28%) is an ambitious but achievable 12-point gain with focused effort.

#### Strategic Focus: The Bottom A5% in Math

Students in the bottom quartile must meet their individualized growth targets, which means they need:

(1) a known, specific growth target, (2) instruction specifically aimed at their identified skill deficits, (3) more frequent progress monitoring than other students, and (4) someone who owns accountability for their growth. The action steps below are designed to build exactly that system.
