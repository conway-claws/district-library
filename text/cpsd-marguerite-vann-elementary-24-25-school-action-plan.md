# Marguerite Vann Elementary School School Action Plan

# 2024-2025

## Area of Concern/Need (#1)

Spring 2023 ACT Aspire data and Spring 2023 NWEA MAP Growth data demonstrates that although academic achievement improved over the previous year, many Vann students are still performing below grade level expectations in reading, English, writing, and/or mathematics. When Spring 2024 data (ATLAS and NWEA) are available, they will be analyzed in order to get a more up-to-date and accurate picture of this area of need.

## Goal

Sixty percent (60%) or more of Vann 3rd and 4th grade students will score proficient in reading and math as measured by the ATLAS assessment given in Spring 2025. Sixty percent (60%) or more of Vann K-2 students will score proficient in reading and math as measured by the new K-2 state assessment given in Spring 2025.

## Strategies

- Adopt a master schedule that prioritizes weekly collaboration time for lesson internalization and delivery of high quality instruction, including designated periods for Tier 1 literacy (whole group and small group), math, and science instruction, as well as Tier 2 & 3 instruction daily.
- Continue utilizing Professional Learning Communities (PLC) practices, such as weekly grade level team collaboration about what students should be learning, how learning will be measured, and specific

teacher responses when students are successful and not successful in learning

- Monitor student-level data weekly with common formative assessments of priority learning targets and respond with appropriate Tier 2 interventions for all students
- Provide teachers with specific expectations for Tier 1 and Tier 2 instruction and utilize instructional coaching to improve teachers’ professional practice
- Promote family engagement in students’ learning including emphasizing that student attendance is a primary way parents/caregivers can impact their student’s academic achievement, as well as encouraging attendance at school family nights and other events
## Professional Development

- All grade level classroom teachers will receive professional development in the the district’s high quality instructional materials (HQIM) this summer
- Staff will take part in a book study of The Will to Learn, which emphasizes the role of teacher credibility and student motivation in learning
- Staff will have the opportunity to self-select professional development experiences that meet their needs and interests relevant to instruction and learning
- All staff will receive training in PLC strategies & instructional best practices during back-to-school professional development days, and this training will continue throughout the school year as the model is implemented
- All grade level classroom teachers will receive feedback and coaching in providing Tier 1 and Tier 2 literacy (whole group and small group) and mathematics instruction, with an emphasis on high quality Tier 1 instruction and building capacity in identifying learning gaps and designing and providing interventions to remediate these gaps
## Implementation Plan

A comprehensive master schedule will be adopted that designates daily periods for Tier 1 mathematics and literacy (whole group and small group) instruction. The master schedule will also provide for 40-60 minutes of dedicated intervention time for each grade level each day, during which appropriate and targeted Tier 2 and Tier 3 instruction (intervention) or

extensions/enrichment will be provided to all students. The schedule will also provide each grade level teacher team with time for collaborative lesson internalization and data analysis weekly. Instructional leaders will participate in this collaboration. Teachers will focus instruction on curriculum standards that all students must know/be able to do prior to the end of the year (essential or “power” standards). Common formative assessments (CFA) will be administered to track progress toward learning targets. Teachers will analyze CFA data and use it to plan and carry out interventions. Administrators will provide clear expectations for planning and instruction (Tier 1 and 2) and observe and provide feedback/coaching in order to assist teachers in maximizing student learning.

## Evaluation

Evaluation of the goal of improved instruction and learning outcomes occurs naturally as a part of the PLC process. Data on students’ progress toward learning targets is gathered and analyzed weekly, and used to plan subsequent instruction. Interim and formative assessment data and state assessments will also be analyzed to determine progress toward the overall goal. A “Guiding Coalition” of staff and instructional leaders will oversee the implementation process and provide evaluation feedback, which will be used to refine the process of instructional improvement.

## <u>Area of Concern/Need (#2)</u>

Vann has an agreed upon set of clearly articulated and shared behavioral expectations and accountability systems that encourage students to demonstrate the expected behaviors. Although notable improvements have been made in student behavior and the overall safety and security of the school environment, some students still struggle with interpersonal/peer relationships and a lack of problem solving, conflict resolution, and other social-emotional coping skills. Improvement and continued refining of systems for the prevention of behavior problems and strategic intervention when problems do occur would allow more time at school to be spent on instruction and thereby increase student achievement toward learning targets. Continuing to support students’ social-emotional wellbeing will enable them to benefit from the academic instruction provided by the school. Similarly, reducing the number of infractions that result in students being removed from the learning environment will also increase student learning.

## Goal

Vann will refine and continue implementation of a multi-tiered, school-wide positive behavior support system. As a result, most behavior problems will

be prevented and effective interventions will be put in place for students who do not meet behavior expectations. Quantitatively, the number of office referrals for which students are suspended from class or school will be reduced by 50%. Additionally, students will be supported in their social-emotional development through core instruction in prosocial behavior. All of these efforts will result in maximizing academic learning time and increased student achievement.

## Strategies

- Provide Tier 1 core instruction in prosocial behavior, including lessons on school-wide behavior expectations as well as developmentally appropriate topics related to social-emotional wellbeing
- Refine and implement with fidelity staff-designed systems for holding students accountable for meeting behavioral expectations
- Develop a schoolwide program to recognize students who demonstrate expected behaviors and celebrate school-wide progress toward prosocial behavior goals
- Provide strategic Tier 2 interventions for students who do not respond to Tier 1 core instruction in behavior
- Effectively utilize data from Functional Behavior Assessments and available Tier 3 behavioral supports for students who need comprehensive services
## Professional Development

- During back-to-school PD, staff will review school-wide behavior expectations and refine and develop systems for encouraging students to demonstrate these behaviors
- During back-to-school PD, staff will receive a refresher in Character Counts, the school’s Tier 1 prosocial behavior curriculum, and integration of the pillars of character into the school team or house system
- Staff will take part in a book study of The Will to Learn, which emphasizes the role of teacher credibility and student motivation in learning, which in turn promotes appropriate school behavior
- Staff will have the opportunity to self-select professional development experiences that meet their needs and interests relevant to classroom management
- Novice teachers will be provided with coaching related to establishing classroom routines and procedures and establishing rapport with students in order to set them up for maximum success

in managing student behavior in the early years of their teaching career

## Implementation Plan

School staff will review and refine shared behavior expectations and systems to acknowledge appropriate and inappropriate behavior. Teachers will provide instruction in prosocial behavior and social-emotional skills, utilizing the school’s selected curriculum, Character Counts. Lessons will be reinforced with opportunities for practice, feedback, and reteaching. Teachers will implement with fidelity the building systems for recognizing and responding to appropriate and inappropriate behavior, including Tier 2 and 3 interventions when needed. Administrators will support teachers by providing ongoing training and time for weekly RtI collaboration, which will include both academic and behavior data.

## Evaluation

Data from weekly RtI collaboration, classroom observations, and discipline referrals will be monitored by the principal, assistant principal, and counselor. All data will be used to plan subsequent Tier 1 instruction and Tier 2 interventions in behavior and social-emotional skills.
