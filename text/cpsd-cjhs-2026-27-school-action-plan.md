# School Improvement Plan 2026/2027

<u>Area of Concern</u> The outcomes of the 2024/25 ATLAS assessment shed light on a notable concern within Conway Public Schools, particularly regarding English Language Arts (ELA). Analysis of the data indicated that the level 3 and 4 categories for 8th grade students is above the state average at 38% and the state average at 33%. Also, Analysis of the data indicated that the level 3 and 4 categories for 9th grade students is well above the state average at 44% and the state average at 31%. However, our area of focus will be our Growth for the Lowest 25%. In ELA, our 8th grade students were at 63% with the state average being at 66%. In ELA, our 9th grade students were at 56% with the state average being at 69%. This indicates that focusing on support for this particular subset will require additional support and resources to enhance their proficiency in this subject area. Recognizing the importance of strong ELA skills for overall academic success, Conway Public Schools is poised to address this challenge through targeted interventions and initiatives aimed at bolstering student achievement in English Language Arts. Conway Junior High School School Improvement Plan 2026-2027

<u>Area of Concern</u> Conway Junior High is committed to providing an inclusive, safe, and supportive learning environment where everyone is valued. However, student conflicts—both physical and verbal—can pose a significant barrier to achieving this mission by distracting students and limiting their ability to fully engage in the learning process. These disruptions not only interrupt instruction but also contribute to a negative school culture, ultimately affecting the overall educational experience. Additionally, valuable instructional time is often lost when attention must be redirected from teaching to conflict resolution. Recognizing the seriousness of this issue, school administrators, staff, and parents have made addressing student conflict a top priority. Through continued collaboration and proactive intervention, the school community can foster a more positive, respectful environment where all students are empowered to succeed academically and socially.

<u>Goal</u> Throughout the 2026–2027 academic year, Conway Junior High School (CJHS) is committed to implementing strategies that reduce student conflicts each quarter relative to the corresponding quarters in 2025. By achieving this reduction, CJHS anticipates a corresponding decrease in both in-school and out-of-school suspensions, consequently fostering an environment more conducive to student achievement. This proactive approach towards mitigating conflicts not only enhances the overall school climate but also aligns with the school's broader mission of promoting academic excellence and holistic student development.

<u>Strategies</u>

- Introduction of Act 1084 initiatives at CJHS
- Implementation of an incentive program to promote positive behaviors among students
- Recognition and rewards for exemplary conduct
- Categories such as Cat Chow, Colossal Cats, and Cha Cha Cats to encourage continuous improvement
- Fresh start after each event to enable students to refocus on positive behavior
- Delivery of teacher-led lessons on conflict resolution to equip students with essential skills

- Introduction of a citizenship training program for students involved in conflicts, emphasizing responsible behavior and community participation
- Launch of the "See Something/Say Something" campaign to encourage students to report potential acts of violence or threats on the CJHS campus
- Implementation of conflict resolution systems across our building.
<u>Professional Development</u> Teachers will need professional development on conflict resolution lessons. This professional development will occur in August of 2026 and will be facilitated by the CJHS administrative staff and instructional facilitators. Sessions will also be available at Conway Public School District PD Palooza.

<u>Implementation Plan</u>

- Jasper Johnson, Rebekah Dixon, Ben Broyles, Steven Lamb, Breanna Taylor & Jennifer Dozler will be responsible for this goal’s implementation and progress monitoring.
- CJHS counselors Deanna Robbins, Nicolette Templeton, Tina Lampe, & Monica Williams will facilitate the citizenship training program.
- CJHS administration will train students on the conflict resolution program as a whole during the first week of school through an assembly, during WIN time, and multiple other occasions.
- CJHS administration will be responsible for assigning students to citizenship training as conflicts occur.
- Progress monitoring will occur through pulling discipline data from eschoolPLUS, citizenship training observations, and classroom observations.
<u>Evaluation</u> Goal success will be evaluated at the end of the 2026/27 school year.

- CJHS administration will use discipline data from eSchoolPLUS to determine the change in the number of conflicts during the 2026/27 school year.
- We will monitor and adjust program components as needed based on an evaluation of the program.
