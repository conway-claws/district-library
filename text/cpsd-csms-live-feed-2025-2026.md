Immunization Reminder:The Arkansas State Board of Health Rules and Regulations Pertaining to Immunization Requirements states that every child attending a public school in the state shall receive a series of immunizations in order to enter school. ### 2025-09-25 · Carl Stuart Middle School (id 10059326)

Immunization Deadline: OCTOBER 1, 2025

The Arkansas State Board of Health Rules and Regulations Pertaining to Immunization Requirements states that every child attending a public school in the state shall receive a series of immunizations in order to enter school. Religious, Medical, and Philosophical exemptions can be obtained through the Arkansas Department of Health. However, proof of exemption letters must be obtained each year and provided to the school for their records. (AR Code Annotated 20-7-109, 6-18-702, 6-60-504, 20-78-206).

If your child is already 11 years old or will turn 11 yrs. before September 1, 2025, he/she will need the Tdap and the MCV4 vaccines before returning to school this year. If your child is entering 7th grade and has not received the MCV4 vaccine, he/she must have this before returning to school this year. 

The deadline for receiving these immunizations is October 1, 2025. If your child does not have the required immunizations by this date, he/she will be sent home until immunizations are updated. There will be NO exceptions! 

All updated records must be turned in to Nurse Siegel. 

Any questions or concerns should be sent to Nurse Siegel at siegelh@conwayschools.info. Or, please call 501-329-2782 with any questions. Thank you!

### 2026-02-17 · Carl Stuart Middle School (id 11403613)

💙 TEACHER FEATURE #4 💙

Mrs. Masters |  5th Grade Reading |  Questioning Protocols

During a recent read-aloud of Crash, students engaged in purposeful questioning to deepen their understanding of the text. As we read, students paid close attention to the questions that naturally arose, helping them remain focused and actively engaged in the story.

We also examined question stems and discussed how different types of questions lead to different levels of thinking. Students learned that while some questions clarify basic understanding, others encourage deeper analysis, inference, and reflection. By intentionally using higher-level question stems, students were able to think more critically about characters, motivations, and events in the novel.

This strategy reinforced that asking thoughtful questions is an essential reading skill—one that builds curiosity, strengthens comprehension, and supports deeper cognitive engagement with complex texts.

Mrs. Wanamaker | 6th Grade Reading | Metaphors

 I used activity 8, Metaphor, as an activity to go with “The Jacket” by Gary Soto. We created metaphors by comparing the main character, Gary, to ordinary items from the classroom. To prepare we discussed how metaphors find similarities between two unlike things. We also talked about the characteristics of the main character using the STEAL method.

Odie Phillips is the Pastor/Teacher of the Greater Fellowship Christian Church of Conway, Arkansas.  He has an extensive background and training in counseling and public relations. He has also served as the former President of the Central Arkansas Ministerial Alliance and currently serves as President of the Fellowship of Christian Pastors and Ministers Alliance.

He received a congratulatory certificate and recognition from the Arkansas Senate and a proclamation from the City of Conway’s Mayor, Tab Townsell, who declared May 26, 2009 as “Odie Phillips Day.”

Odie Phillips is a Man of God with a vision for the 21st Century; he teaches the Word of God with authority that enables and empowers God’s people to mature to higher spiritual levels; and he has evangelized under the power and the anointing of the Holy Spirit from Coast to Coast. ### 2026-03-10 · Carl Stuart Middle School (id 11664164)

⭐ TEACHER FEATURE #8 ⭐

Mr. German | Health/PE | Adding Movement to Learning

Mrs. Wanamaker and I collaborated on this lesson to combine movement with learning by creating a review game focused on text structures in reading. Students used scooters to move around the room and find their assigned color card, which contained a short passage of text. They then had to evaluate the passage, determine which of the five text structures it represented, and explain their reasoning to either Mrs. Wanamaker or me. If their answer and explanation were correct, they placed their passage on the gallery wall under the matching text structure category.

This process continued until all passages were correctly identified. We played two rounds of the game. Although we ran out of time, the planned closure was for the groups to participate in a gallery walk, reading each passage and discussing why it fit its assigned text structure category.

Ms. Kronberg | 5th Grade Math |  Math Vocabulary to Build Understanding

When starting our module on fractions, we decided it was important to focus on vocabulary to help students build strong background knowledge. Students first worked with a definition sheet that introduced key fraction terms. They then used those definitions to complete an activity page where they had to identify the type of fraction being described. ### 2026-03-31 · Carl Stuart Middle School (id 11860734)

⭐ TEACHER FEATURE #12 ⭐

Mrs. Mosley | 5th Grade ELA | Adding Movement to Learning

This strategy encourages students to physically move as they learn new concepts, which helps keep them engaged and focused. I used this approach to get my students up and active while introducing the structure of an informational essay. To make the parts more memorable, I taught them a chant paired with purposeful hand motions that represented each part of the essay—such as the introduction, the thesis statement, body, and the conclusion. Students practiced the chant with partners, allowing them to rehearse the sequence, offer each other feedback, and build confidence with the movements. This interactive routine not only strengthened their understanding of the essay’s parts and their functions, but also provided a multisensory tool they can rely on to recall the structure during the writing process.

### 2026-05-05 · Carl Stuart Middle School (id 12224994)

TEACHER FEATURE #21

Mrs. Charton | 7th Grade ELA | Think Pair Square Share

The goal of this activity was to support students in developing strong research questions for an expository research essay through collaboration and consensus building. To begin the process, students independently created research questions based on specific characteristics that were provided and recorded them in individual boxes. They then shared their questions with an elbow partner at their table, allowing for initial feedback and discussion. Next, pairs joined with another pair across from them to form small groups, where they continued sharing and evaluating their questions. Within these groups, students discussed and agreed upon the strongest research question, which they recorded in the center box. Finally, each group shared their selected question with the class, and I collected them on a Google Slide in real time so that students could reflect on the examples and revise or replace weaker questions as needed.

### 2026-05-05 · Carl Stuart Middle School (id 12155642)

TEACHER FEATURE #20

Mrs. Irvin | 7th Grade ELA |  Implementing Strategies for Create-Level Thinking

Students read two accounts of the myth of Icarus: a retelling of the myth “The Flight of Icarus” by Sally Benson and a poem “Icarus’s Flight” by Stephen Dobyns. They were then tasked with writing 3 paragraphs comparing and contrasting the two versions of the story. Before they began writing, I had them brainstorm the similarities and differences by using this Hits and Spots Strategy. I gave student groups (ranging from 3-6 students) a set of sticky notes and a sheet of “dots” (stars). The students started by writing any similarities and differences they could think of on the sticky notes. Then, they analyzed each other’s ideas and put dots on the ideas (sticky notes) they felt were most effective and most relevant. Next, they grouped the sticky notes into similarities and differences. Finally, we had a class discussion about their findings and completed a brainstorming page together.
