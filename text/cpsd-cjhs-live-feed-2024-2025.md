### 2025-02-03 · Conway Junior High School (id 8130869)

CJHS will be celebrating Black History Month by highlighting local community members. We will start off with Mr. Tim Ester!

Tim Ester was born and raised in Menifee, Arkansas. He is a graduate of Morrilton High
School. He attended the University of Central Arkansas in Conway, but while in college
Tim worked a full time job that eventually became a high priority in his life. From that
point forward Tim found his profession in the restaurant business and he is best known
as the General Manager of Stoby’s Restaurant where he served for 26 years before
retiring at the early age of 49.

Tim is also well known for his service to the community that all began with his work on
the United Way Board of Directors. Through Stoby’s Restaurant, Tim was also
instrumental in supporting many, many causes throughout the city of Conway in the
pancake breakfast fundraiser. In 2008 Tim co-founded the Pine Street Backpack
Program, a non-profit that supports education by providing school supplies to students
in need in grades K-12 that live in Faulkner County. Over the past 16 years the Pine
Street Backpack Program has raised nearly $370,000 and served more than 14,000
students.

In 2019 Tim had a vision to open a food truck. After considerable thought and a great
deal of research, he started the process by first special ordering his dream kitchen that
would be manufactured in the state of Georgia.

When COVID hit and businesses were shut down, plans to open this small business
was stalled. What appeared to be the worst time to open a business, while others were
closing as a result of the pandemic, was actually the opportune time. As people could
no longer dine inside of restaurants, they looked for environments that accommodated
outdoor seating and curbside pickup.

In June of 2020 Uncle Tim’s Food Truck was established; and today it is a staple in the
community. Located at the corner of Highway 286 East and Skunk Hollow Road, on a
historic site owned by his wife’s family, and formerly Highway 286 East Grocery and
Gas.

Tim and his wife, Gena Ester, have been married 36 years. The two work hand in hand
in their family’s business operations of running a food truck and catering company as
well as managing a number of rental properties. They have two children, Kiera Oluokun
(Seyi) and Jordan Ester (Kirstan). The Esters are also proud grandparents of two
handsome boys, Ezekiel (7) and Isaac (3).

Conway Junior High Celebrates: Torrie Achan

Torrie C. Achan was born in Little Rock, Arkansas. She is the oldest child of Agnes Stewart-Hill and the late Elihu Stewart Jr. She has three siblings. She graduated from J.A. Fair High School in 1996. She attended Henderson State University and received a Bachelor of Science in Education with an Emphasis in Early Childhood in 2000. While at Henderson, she was initiated into the Theta Eta Chapter of Alpha Kappa Alpha Sorority, Incorporated in Fall 1998. Alpha Kappa Alpha Sorority, Incorporated is the oldest Greek-letter organization founded by African- American college-educated women. Also, while at Henderson, she met and married her college sweetheart, Joey Achan. In December 2002, she received a Master of Science in Education from the University of Central Arkansas.

Torrie started her teaching career in Conway Public Schools in 2000. She taught at Theodore Jones Elementary from 2000-2010. While at Theodore Jones Elementary, she taught fourth and first grades. In 2010, she received a position at Woodrow Cummins Elementary School where she is currently serving as a teacher. From 2010-2023, she served as a third grade teacher. Currently, she is teaching fourth grade math and science. In May 2025, she will complete her 25th year as a classroom teacher. Torrie feels blessed beyond measure because she has worked with so many wonderful administrators, teachers, support staff, parents, and students during her career. Each day when she enters her classroom, her goal is to be the teacher that I would want for my personal children to encounter each day..

Outside of work, Torrie enjoys spending time with family and friends, reading, working out at the Lyons’ Den Wellness Center, and traveling. Also, she loves serving in various ministries at Rock Solid Church. 

Torrie is married to Joey Achan, a fellow Conway Public Schools Educator. In December 2024, they celebrated 25 years of marriage. They have two wonderful children, Madison (20) and Josiah (15) and a fun little Maltese-mix named Marble.

Conway Junior High Highlights: Dr. Corey Oliver

Dr. Corey Oliver’s teaching career began at Bob Courtway (now Bob and Betty Courtway)
Middle School in 1997, the year the school opened. He is now in his 28 th year of teaching there. Dr. Oliver graduated from the University of Central Arkansas with a Bachelor of Arts (BA) degree in English in 1996. In 2000, he completed his Master of Science in Education (MSE) degree in Secondary School Leadership, also at UCA. In 2021, he earned a Doctor of Philosophy (PhD) degree from UCA’s Department of Leadership Studies. His dissertation is titled “The Role of School Leaders in Addressing Discipline and Developing an Equitable Organizational Culture”.

Dr. Oliver approaches each teaching opportunity with a singular focus: to engage every
member of the class in a productive learning experience, accommodating the students’ diverse interests and learning styles through effective teaching strategies while fulfilling the lesson’s accompanying goals and objectives. Whether it be through the involvement of students in decision making or through individualized learning, he enthusiastically engages students to maximize each student’s learning potential. Someone once said, “Tell me and I forget, teach me and I may remember, involve me and I learn.” Involving students in their learning and believing in each student’s ability to set and achieve goals through rigor, relevance, and relationship, Dr. Oliver has encouraged his students to “Strive for Excellence!”, his class motto since 1997. Throughout his 28 years as a classroom teacher, Dr. Oliver has not only strived to involve his students in the learning process, but he has also engaged in routine reflection to grow from previous practice and strived to fulfill his obligation to make a positive, lasting impact on each student in this rewarding work of heart—teaching!

Dr. Oliver is a Certified Candidate Support Provider for the National Board for Professional
Teaching Standards (NBPTS), a consultant for the Arkansas Department of Education’s Arkansas Professional Pathway to Educator Licensure (APPEL) program, and a former adjunct instructor in UCA’s College of Education.

Passionate about international travel, Dr. Oliver has participated in international training
programs for educators in Russia, China, Japan, and South Africa. In all, he has traveled to 45 countries in his quest to experience other cultures and to increase others’ cultural appreciation.

Dr. Oliver’s greatest inspirations are his parents, Johnnie and Evon Oliver, who passed away 33 days apart in 2022. Their support was constant, and their love was unconditional. 

Dr. Oliver’s career highlights include:
- Conway Public Schools Foundation Teacher of the Week (2024)
- Arvest Bank “We Love Teachers” Award (2022)
- National Board Certified Teacher (2006 & 2015)
- Teacher of the Year, Courtway Middle School (2000 & 2014)

Conway Junior High Celebrates: Sadeidre Lawrence

As a ninth grader from the tiny, no-stoplight town of Danville, Sadeidre Lawrence knew on an FBLA field trip to UCA that she wanted to make Conway her home one day. Since her parents had already instilled her with a formidable work ethic thanks to countless hours spent cleaning carpets, waxing floors, and rebuilding flood and fire damaged homes because of the family’s restoration business, Sadeidre knew she would have to apply that same set of principles to her education in order to turn her dream into a reality. 

In 2010, Sadeidre graduated from Danville High School with honors, her freshman year of college already completed, and a full-ride to UCA as an English major. A short time after beginning classes, Sadeidre realized that she wanted to double-major in Sociology and began that coursework as well. While attending college, Sadeidre held several jobs: a paid internship at the Faulkner County Juvenile Court, a seasonal position at Old Navy, and a part-time job at Lowe’s Home Improvement. 

In 2013, after being named the Katherine Hinson Outstanding Sociology Student, Sadeidre graduated magna cum laude with a Bachelor of Arts in English and Sociology. After graduation, she applied for a full-time job at Lowe’s and, over the course of the next two years, worked her way up to a managerial position in 2015. 

Sadeidre knew after working retail for several years that she was ready for a change, and in 2017, she decided to return  to college and temporarily relocate to Mount Ida. Around that time, she became engaged, and while Sadeidre was attending Henderson State University for her Master of Arts in Teaching, she became an English instructor at Mena High School. In 2018, she and her fiancé returned to Conway to purchase their home, and she began teaching 9th grade at Benton Junior High. In November of 2018, Sadeidre married her husband, Matthew, in Conway, and in early 2019, she was hired by the Conway Junior High to teach 8th grade English.

### 2025-06-23 · Conway Public Schools (id 9409541)

SPECIAL RECORDS TO BE DESTROYED
On July 7, 2025, the Conway Public School District will destroy Special Education records of students who graduated, moved or were dismissed from Special Education services in the district between January 1, 2019 and June 30, 2019. This process is carried out in compliance with federal and state laws. Students, parents and/or guardians may obtain any data from folders prior to July 7, 2024, by calling the Special Education office at (501) 450-6634, extension 0303 or emailing at records@conwayschools.info.
