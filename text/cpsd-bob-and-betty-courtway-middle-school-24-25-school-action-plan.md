# Courtway Middle School School Action Plan

# 2024-2025

## <u>GOAL 1 - Reading</u>

## Area of Need/Concern

- Amount of students who read on or above grade level.
**Data**

- **In Spring of 2023, the percent of students who are READING at or above grade** **level on the ACT Aspire building-wide was 35.8%**
## Measurable Goal

- Increase the percentage of students from not achieving to achieving in each grade level by 7% by the end of the 2024/25 school year.
## Strategies

1. Continue the implementation of the Science of Reading Initiative (RISE). o Include an increased focus on literacy across all classes, with planned
access to reading in all disciplines. o Focus on morphological practices for vocabulary words in core content classes.

2. For the 2024/25 school year, we will continue implementing Into Reading by HMH for 5th and 6th Grade and Into Literature by HMH for 7th Grade, focusing on using learning ladders for essential learning goals. o Utilize close reading strategies, as they are embedded through this
curricular resource. o Standardize the use of learning ladders across all reading and ELA courses, placing learning goals in the hands of students for a better understanding of skills and need for mastery. o Track mastery of essential standards using CFA data and learning ladders. o Create a data wall for student reading levels by the reading team to track student progress using through-the-year data.

3. Implement RTI practices for students needing Tier 2 and Tier 3 interventions.
4. Continue use of Sonday interventions as Tier III interventions.
2024-25 CMS School Action Plan Page 1

5. Build a more robust culture of reading by incentivizing and celebrating reading growth.
## Professional Development

- We will continue training and implementing Marzano’s High Yield strategies for all teachers, especially those new to CMS and CPSD.
- We will continue implementing instructional strategies for reading in each content area, primarily through our work with *Notice and Note* by Beers and Probst and morphological practices from RISE training.
- Continue to support and train Orton-Gillingham interventionists as needed.
- Implement classroom tours and observations for staff members using specific and successful reading strategies.
## Implementation Plan

- RISE strategies—Staff will continue training on using RISE strategies in the classroom. All teachers will continue to implement RISE strategies to build a school-wide focus on literacy skills in each classroom, especially morphological practices for teachers in all classes.
- Marzano’s High Yield Strategies- instructional facilitators will provide PD to staff on Marzano’s HYS in August 2024. Staff will implement targeted use of strategies identified for use in professional development.
- We will utilize our middle school IFs to enhance our implementation of reading strategies from *Notice and Note* across the content areas.
- Reading IF and team of CMS teachers will plan school-wide incentives and celebrations based on STAR reading growth, reading challenges, and Read Across America in March. ●
## Evaluation

- As we meet in varying teams/groups, we will identify if our students are making appropriate gains. o ACT Aspire o STAR reading results o CFA and Curriculum Assessment Data o Reading Assessment Data (Reading Inventory by HMH) o Interim assessment data o Intervention data

## GOAL 2 - ACADEMIC INTERVENTION

## Area of Need/Concern

- Number of students that need to perform at grade level in Literacy or Math.
## Data

- ACT Aspire data from the Spring of 2023 shows 25% of CMS students score In Need of Support in math, and nearly 39% of CMS students score In Need Of Support in ELA.
- The same ACT Aspire data results show that 39% of students score close in math, while nearly 22% score close in ELA.

|ACT Aspire Achievement 22-23|# of students (math)|% of students (math)|# of students (ELA)|% of students (ELA)|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|In Need of Support|127|25.1%|195|38.7%|
|Close|197|39.0%|109|21.6%|
|Ready|135|26.7%|111|22.0%|
|Exceeding|46|9.1%|89|17.7%|

## Measurable Goal

- We will decrease the percentage of CMS students who score In Need of Support on summative assessment data by 10% while increasing the percentage of students at Ready or above by 5%.
## Strategies

1. We will identify all students who need Tier 2 and Tier 3 intervention. Regarding those students, we will monitor their progress throughout the year. o Continue using RTI scheduler to place students into intervention sessions
for academic needs.

2. Core staff members will use their common prep time to plan instruction, review data, and plan interventions. o Staff expectation of meetings twice a month with an academic facilitator
to discuss planning and intervention needs. (Review instruction, assessment, and data.)

3. We will shift the focus of the building academic intervention team to a student intervention team (SIT) to identify and plan student intervention needs. The SIT team will meet monthly and build a referral process for teachers.
4. All core staff members will use learning ladders to focus on skills and common formative assessments to focus on student data in their classes.
5. CMS staff and IFs will build a 5th-grade curriculum for a “wheel” intervention class to teach students high-utility, highly transferable skills. o Students will work on study skills, math fluency, literacy skills, and STEM
skills. **Professional Development**

- Curricular teams will offer PD in the summer of 2024 to prepare learning ladders and CFAs for use in core classes.
- PD will be offered in summer of 2024 for teachers to build an intervention curriculum for the intervention “wheel.”
- Ongoing learning and reflection on RTI practices at staff meetings.
- All general education and special education teachers will receive accommodation/modification training in August of 2024 to help meet diverse student needs in Tier 1 and Tier 2 instruction.
## Implementation Plan

- Use of data-driven team meetings, RtI meetings, and PLCs.
- We will continue to use build-in school-wide intervention time while modifying the current 5th-grade intervention class to include the new intervention “wheel” curriculum.
- We will continue to provide Tier III and targeted intervention for students struggling to read and perform math on grade level.
- Planned data-driven common prep meetings with IFs on the calendar for all staff members.
## Evaluation

- We will determine whether our intervention students are making appropriate gains as we meet with grade-level and content-specific teams. o Classroom assessment data o Interim assessment data o STAR assessment data o Intervention data o Classroom anecdotal data o Progress monitoring data

## <u>GOAL 3 - BEHAVIORAL INTERVENTION</u>

## Area of Need/Concern

- **** Raising Student Achievement and Behavioral Expectations among all faculty members and students.
## Data

- Based on CMS staff anecdotal data and referral data, increased behavioral support is needed to meet the needs of our students and staff. Our staff began implementing school-wide behavioral expectations using the C-WAY matrix during the 2022/23 school year and added Tier 2 and Tier 3 practices to help those students who need more significant support in 2023/24.
## Measurable Goal

- Decrease office referrals and disciplinary actions assigned to students. o Decrease ISS and OSS total days by 20%.
- Increase in positive office referrals
- Increase student attendance rate.
## Strategies

1. Continued implementation of School-Wide Positive Behavior Supports (SWPBS).
a. A collaborative effort to establish values and expectations across the campus
b. Behavior Matrix-C-WAY(Choose kindness; Wise Choices; Act Safely; Yes, I Can!)
c. Tier 1 behavioral instruction to implement throughout the different school settings
d. Teachers, administrators, and counselors will provide student rewards and recognition opportunities based on meeting behavioral matrix goals.
2. Formation of CMS Behavioral Intervention Team and a school-wide behavior team to address consistency and solutions for staff members. These teams will focus on RTI practices for behavior and strategies/interventions CMS can use to support student behavioral solutions while also reflecting on whole-school practices that can be strengthened.
3. Increased communication and collaboration among staff on student behavior issues using behavioral RTI ideals.
4. Strong focus on traits for successful students and SEL skills.
5. Build smaller, more frequent celebrations and incentives for students based on positive behavior.

## Professional Development

- In August 2024, teachers will be provided training on classroom and whole-school behavior practices to address staff needs. Expectations, consistency, relationships, and standard practices will be taught and planned for the 2024/25 school year. o Follow-up training will be provided virtually for all staff.
- All staff members will be encouraged to attend summer PD sessions at AFESC on behavior to bring back learning for our staff.
- *Thrivers* book study PD will be ongoing throughout the school year.
## Implementation Plan

- Continue to teach the Tier 1 behavior matrix and school-wide behavioral procedures. Focus on ways to positively correct misbehavior using deescalation and questions. o Meet with the committee in May of 2024 to plan more frequent, smaller
incentives and celebrations for positive student behavior.

- Monthly focus on a trait from *Thrivers* book study
- Weekly focused SEL lesson during WIN time for all students on Mondays built by CMS staff members.
- Formation of a Behavioral RTI team. The team will continue to learn about behavioral RTI and serve our school staff in this role when students need greater support. o The team will provide grade-level teams with strategies and interventions
for students needing further behavior support. o A school-wide procedures and expectations tracking system will be created and shared with all CMS staff members. o Assistant principals and counselors will continue to hold monthly behavioral meetings with each team (5, 6, & 7).

## Evaluation

- As we meet during leadership team meetings and faculty meetings, we will analyze the data we collect regarding behavior RTI. (Referrals, interventions, attendance, etc.)
