### 2025-05-15 · Kerri Wofford (id 9191088)

Today, we are wearing purple on MPS Awareness Day to show support for families impacted by child neurodegenerative disorders, Mucopolysaccharidosis or MPS. 

MPS diseases are rare genetic disorders that are present at birth, although symptoms may not manifest until early childhood before worsening progressively. Such symptoms can affect different parts of the body including airway passages, ears, facial features, the skeleton and joints, eyes, the liver, the heart, and in many cases, the brain.

An example of an MPS disease is Sanfilippo Syndrome - also known as Mucopolysaccharidosis type Ill or MPS III - is a terminal, neurodegenerative, rare disease. It causes children to lose all the skills they've gained, suffer seizures and movement disorders, experience pain and suffering, and then die, often before the second decade of life.

Because of its neurodegenerative nature and multi-system impact, Sanfilippo Syndrome is often called "childhood Aizheimer's." It is classified as a form of childhood dementia.

There is not currently an FDA treatment or cure for Sanfillipo Syndrome.
