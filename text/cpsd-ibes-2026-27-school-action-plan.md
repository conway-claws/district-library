# Ida Burns Elementary School

## 2026–2027 School Action Plan

#### Conway Public School District

# Plan Overview

This School Action Plan aligns Ida Burns Elementary School's 2026–2027 improvement priorities directly with the Conway Public School District's Strategic Reading Plan and Math Plan. The district's overarching goals are to raise reading proficiency from 41% to 80% by 2029–2030 and math proficiency from 38% to 60% or higher by

2030. As a building entering Year 2 of the district's five-year literacy initiative, Ida Burns will build on Year 1 foundations—professional development in the Science of Reading (SoR), adoption of HQIM programs (Arts & Letters, 95% Phonics), and intervention restructuring—while expanding implementation fidelity, data literacy, and family engagement.

### Area of Concern #1: Literacy

Background: As of 2024–2025, 41% of Conway students in grades 3–10 read on grade level. The district's Strategic Reading Plan identifies fragmented literacy instruction, inconsistent intervention response, and limited family engagement as key barriers. Year 1 investments included SoR professional development, pilot adoption of a new HQIM, Take Flight/Rite Flight/Build training, and establishment of literacy coaching models. In 2026–2027, Ida Burns will deepen these investments under Year 2 of the district plan.

**Goal #1: By the end of the 2026–2027 school year, 60% of Ida Burns students will have met** **their projected literacy growth goal, as measured by the 2026–2027 ATLAS Summative** **Assessment.**

Alignment: District Strategic Reading Plan — Year 2 goals include updating the strategic plan, analyzing Tier 3 intervention data, SoR professional development, and continuing data analysis PLCs. This building goal directly supports the district's trajectory of +8 percentage points per year toward 80% proficiency by 2029–2030.

## <u>Literacy Strategies & Action Steps</u>

##### 1. High-Quality Instructional Materials (HQIM) Implementation

- Teachers will fully implement Benchmark Reading (comprehension and fluency) and 95% Phonics (foundational skills) as outlined in district unit plans with fidelity.
- Grades K–4 will implement RISE with full use of decodable texts targeting specific reading levels.
- Teachers will attend district Curriculum Planning days to build knowledge of pacing and instructional routines for all Tier 1 materials.
- The instructional coach will participate in district-level planning sessions aligned to SoR and adopted HQIMs.
##### 2. Science of Reading & Professional Development

- All teachers will attend district-facilitated professional development aligned to SoR and HQIM implementation throughout the school year. (Alignment: District Year 2 — PD for teachers not yet trained in HQIM; ongoing SoR PD.)
- The instructional coach will facilitate weekly grade-level PLC meetings to support lesson internalization, common formative assessment review, and data-driven planning.
- Administrators and the instructional coach will conduct regular classroom walkthroughs using DESE key levers for instructional leaders across the five literacy impact areas.
- Walkthrough data will be reviewed with teachers to drive instructional improvement and coaching cycles.
##### 3. Tiered Intervention & RTI

- All classroom teachers will provide daily Tier II intervention during RTI time, targeting specific phonological awareness and phonics gaps identified through screening data.
- School interventionists will provide pull-out Tier II and Tier III intervention; Tier III students will be served using district-adopted Scottish Rite programs (Build for K–2; Rite Flight for grade 3+) as training and readiness allow.
- The RTI team will analyze data on struggling students every 4–6 weeks to determine progress and adjust intervention assignments. (Alignment: District Year 2 — Analyze Tier 3 intervention data.)
- Teachers will maintain Data Notebooks containing anecdotal notes, checklists, accuracy checks, screener results, and informal assessment measures.
##### 4. Assessment & Progress Monitoring

- Screeners (Beginning Decoding Survey, dyslexia screeners, DIBELS/Amplify) will be administered at the beginning of the year and at regular intervals to identify at-risk students early.
- Interim assessments and progress monitoring data will be reviewed collaboratively by teachers, the instructional coach, and administrators to inform instructional decisions.
- A Quarterly Data Wall will be maintained and used for RTI team discussions.
- Based on student need, administrators and the instructional facilitator will identify additional teacher professional learning to close instructional gaps.
##### 5. Family & Community Engagement

- Teachers will communicate regularly with families to support reading instruction at home, sharing practical literacy strategies.
- The media specialist and literacy interventionist will organize and host a Guest Reading Day for community members and families. (Target: March 2027.)
- The building will participate in district-hosted Family Literacy Nights, including briefings on Atlas reports and literacy strategies. (Alignment: District Year 2 — Literacy Reports given to families each nine weeks / IRPs / Quarterly Progress Reports.)
- Literacy resources will be shared with families via the school website and parent-friendly communications.

# Area of Concern #2: Mathematics

Background: As of 2024–2025, Conway's district-wide math proficiency stands at approximately 38–42% across tested grades, with the district targeting 43% by 2026 and 60%+ by 2030. The district Math Plan identifies foundational skill gaps across five strands (Fractions/Proportional Reasoning, Equation Solving, Data Literacy, Geometry/Spatial Reasoning, and Translating Real-World Problems to Mathematical Models), limited teacher content knowledge in mathematics, and insufficient intervention structures as the primary barriers. Ida Burns will support the district's 2025–2026 and 2026–2027 priorities including HQIM fidelity (Eureka Math²), targeted interventions, Math Intervention Plans (MIPs) for Level 1 ATLAS students, and the development of acceleration pathways.

**Goal #2: By the end of the 2026–2027 school year, 40% of Ida Burns students will have met** **their projected math growth goal, as measured by the 2026–2027 ATLAS Summative** **Assessment.**

Alignment: CPSD Math Plan — district proficiency target is 43% by 2026. The building's 40% growth goal directly supports this trajectory and the five-year plan toward 60%+ by 2030.

## <u>Mathematics Strategies & Action Steps</u>

##### 1. Tier 1 Instruction & HQIM Fidelity

- Teachers in grades K–7 will implement Eureka Math² as the core Tier 1 mathematics program, ensuring alignment with pacing guides and district unit plans.
- Teachers will use Eureka Math Equip diagnostic assessment data to identify prerequisite skill gaps and adjust Tier 1 instruction accordingly.
- Administrators and the instructional coach will use district math look-fors to conduct walkthroughs focused on evidence of HQIM implementation fidelity and effective math instructional practices.
##### 2. Intervention & Math Intervention Plans (MIPs)

- All students who scored Level 1 on the 2025–2026 ATLAS math assessment will receive a Math Intervention Plan (MIP) in compliance with Arkansas Code Annotated § 6-17-431.
- MIPs will target specific skill deficits identified from ATLAS highly missed standards (fractions, equation solving fluency, data literacy, geometry vocabulary, real-world problem translation).
- Intervention periods will be used for targeted Tier 2 small-group instruction based on identified skill deficits and screener data.
- Building leaders and the instructional coach will review and refine intervention time to ensure maximum effectiveness. (Alignment: District 2026–27)
##### 3. Professional Development & Teacher Capacity

- Teachers will attend district-facilitated professional development aligned to math conceptual understanding, vertical alignment, and effective instructional strategies.
- Grade-level PLCs will meet regularly to review formative assessment data, co-plan instruction, and reflect on student progress.
- The instructional coach will support teachers through classroom-embedded coaching cycles, with a focus on math discourse routines (number talks, turn-and-talks, error analysis).
- (Alignment: District 2025–26 and 2026–27 — targeted professional learning; continued partnership for educator readiness in math.)
##### 4. Assessment & Data-Driven Instruction

- Teachers will use Eureka Math Equip to gather real-time formative data, monitor learning by standard, and make timely instructional adjustments.

- ATLAS interim and summative data will be reviewed by grade-level teams with the instructional coach to identify bottom-quartile students and refine intervention plans.
- K–2 screener data will be used to identify students with potential math risk early and provide proactive support. (Building data: 42% of first graders flagged for potential risk — 5 pts above state average.)
- Teachers will maintain MIPs and update them as data warrants throughout the year.
##### 5. Family Communication & Community Engagement

- Teachers will communicate student math progress through regular progress reports and conferences, using parent-friendly language.
- (Alignment: District 2026–27 — Math Resource Page for families with grade-level fluency materials.)
