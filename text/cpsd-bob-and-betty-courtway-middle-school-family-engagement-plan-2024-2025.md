## <u>1: Jointly Developed</u>

Describe how the School works with parents and families to develop and review relevant plans, policies, and strategies related to engagement.

*Guiding Questions*

- ***1.1:** How does the School-in collaboration with parents-establish an engagement plan that* *reflects the specific academic improvement needs of the School, and that includes programs* *and practices that enhance engagement and address the specific engagement needs of* *students and their families?*
[*A.C.A. § 6-15-1702(a)*]

- ***1.2:** What efforts have been made to ensure adequate representation of parents and families* *of participating children in the process (Title I families)?*
[*ESSA § 1116(c)(3)*]

All stakeholders at CMS have a voice and are encouraged to engage actively with the school community. Parents and teachers are invited to join PATHSS (Parents and Teachers Helping Students Succeed), and many play an active role in this organization. We solicit feedback from parents throughout the school year and again when we meet to develop our parental engagement plan.

The parental engagement plan committee included teachers, counselors, communications specialists, and parents representing CMS's diverse families. Furthermore, students gave feedback to both parents and teachers on how CMS could better involve families, students, and community members.

## <u>2: Communication</u>

Describe how the School will communicate with and distribute information to parents and families.

*Guiding Questions*

- ***2.1:** How does the School distribute an informational packet appropriate for the age and grade* *of each child annually, ensuring to include:*

- *description of the engagement program*
- *recommended roles for parents, students, teacher, and the School*
- *ways for a family to get involved*
- *survey regarding volunteer interests*
- *schedule of activities planned throughout the school year*
- *regular, two-way, and meaningful system for parents/teachers to communicate* [*A.C.A. § 6-15-1702(b)(3)(B)(i)*]
- ***2.2:** How will the School ensure information related to school and parent programs, meetings,* *and other activities is provided to parents in a format and in a language that parents can* *understand (to the extent practicable)?*
- *How is relevant information provided in a variety of ways? (For example, paper copies* *made available, as well as social media posts, website links, parent apps, etc.?* [*ESSA § 1116(e)(5)*]
- ***2.3:** How does the School offer flexible opportunities for meetings with families?*
[*ESSA § 1116(c)(2)*]

CMS will host Cat Camp for all incoming 5th-grade families and an Open House for all other families before the school year begins. At these events, a common “Welcome Packet” will be given to families, explaining what it means to be a successful middle school student. This packet will also be provided to all students in the first week of school and those who enroll at CMS throughout the school year. The packet will include strategies to help build literacy at home, access to resources, Schoology help/tips, and Frequently Asked Questions.

Teachers will continue communicating with families using Apptegy and updating all stakeholders regularly in the language they need in their homes. To further this effort this year, CMS will also start a monthly newsletter each grade-level team offers, updating parents on events, academics, and relevant announcements.

This year, CMS will provide parents with academic progress reports every three weeks to keep parents and students up to date with current performance. Teachers will contact parents about their child and offer meetings if necessary. Teachers communicate with individual parents via Apptegy, email, phone, and other means. Furthermore, CMS will host all-day conferences to meet with parents, ensuring all parents have ample opportunity to meet with their child's teachers.

Our Parent/Teacher Organization, PATHSS, proactively connects CMS with community partners to support students effectively. We collect feedback from staff and parents, then organize workshops and meetings tailored to address specific needs. These encompass crucial topics such as school expectations, technology, and student well-being. Additionally, we conduct two annual parent-teacher conferences and guarantee direct communication with parents to promptly and effectively address any concerns. Continuous training for CMS staff on effective communication with students and parents is a priority for achieving student success.

CMS will train CMS staff on engaging parents, including the importance of parental support, at professional development in August. Grade-level teams will be given time to work on their monthly newsletters and taught the best means to share them based on parent feedback.

CMS hosts an orientation for parents and students at the beginning of each school year to inform parents about our curriculum, teacher procedures, and assessment results. Throughout the school year, especially at our school orientation, parents are shown how to use school communication means

to contact teachers, counselors, and staff. CMS provides help and troubleshooting to all parents on orientation nights in the library, with counselors, media specialists, and others providing help. Parents are also taught to use Schoology to check students' progress, grades, and school work regularly. To further our efforts to build parental capacity this year, we will host a parent night outside our typical events to show parents how to partner with their children and CMS.

CMS is planning a book study on *Thrivers* to incorporate the traits of successful adolescents at our school. The school counselor will create a newsletter for parents to summarize a monthly trait and how to build it on their child. An example would be self-confidence. In the month CMS discusses this trait, parents, teachers, students, and staff will all engage in the process. CMS will continue to provide a parent resource center for the 2024/25 school year. The center allows parents to find and use resources to help them support their children's success at CMS.

Our parental organization will continue to discuss how we can share this trait and engage more families at their monthly meetings. PATHSS will also partner with the school by giving feedback, planning initiatives, and helping build a healthy school culture.

The CMS social worker works with community members to connect families to resources. Some resources gathered by CMS staff include access to local state health organizations and social programs that provide for the well-being of our students through parental education (Department of Health). CMS will also host experts at the parent night to share their expertise on subjects that will help parents. The social worker also helps families who need help with their basic needs like food, clothing, and shelter.
